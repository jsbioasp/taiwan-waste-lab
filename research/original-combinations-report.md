# Chain chemistry to trash detection; don't fuse them

No published model performs both of the student's tasks, and the version the student implicitly proposes — one network fed Raman spectra and satellite pixels — is a category error: Raman measures a scattering *shift* in cm⁻¹ on a known object, satellite reflectance measures absorption *depth* in nm over a 10–60 m mixed pixel, and the only vibrational-spectrum fusion that works in the literature (Vib2Mol, Raman + mid-IR) works precisely because both modalities share a wavenumber axis and it explicitly excludes NIR/reflectance ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07014v4)). What does exist, downloadable today, supports three honest designs. The first is a shared-trunk multi-dataset Sentinel-2 debris model in the instructor's own recipe — train on **MARIDA + MADOS (both CC BY 4.0, 1.2 GB + 4.0 GB)** with per-dataset heads and a physically grounded auxiliary target, then score Taiwan coastline segments against the Society of Wilderness bags-per-100 m tables and OCA cleanup CSVs — with Rußwurm 2023's multi-dataset UNet++ and Inzerillo 2025's frozen trunk with a MARIDA head as the closest literature precedents. The second is a cheaper two-stage pipeline where an off-the-shelf detector (the iScience UNet++ on Torch Hub, or the AerialWaste ResNet50+FPN) finds debris and a separate 1-D classifier trained on polymer-labelled reflectance libraries (**MADLib, 24,889 spectra, CC BY 4.0, 210 MB**) or on Raman assigns the material, sharing only a label vocabulary. The third is research-grade hyperspectral work (EMIT/EnMAP/PRISMA with a plastic matched filter, STARCOP-style) that is real but not a novice project. Within each domain the best-supported answers are: for Raman, small 1-D CNNs top the only controlled benchmark, tabular foundation models lead the 77-dataset RamanBench, everything trains in minutes on a T4, inference is sub-millisecond, and the student's "few open datasets" claim is now wrong (**93 loadable datasets, 325,862 spectra**) while "no general pretrained everyday-chemical model" is still right; for waste, dataset design beats architecture — MARIDA's Random Forest still beats its U-Net, the merged-data UNet++ wins the only cross-dataset protocol, Swin-T at 20 cm is the best AerialWaste number (**F1 92.0**), and the student's headline figures (Biermann "86 %", ResAttUNet "0.95", PERIVALLON "90 %", "lightweight models overfit less") are each misread or unsupported. Taiwan has no pixel-labelled satellite waste data and no open Raman data at all; its usable assets are segment-level NGO survey tables, OCA tonnage CSVs, the July-2026 MDImageNet ground/drone set (CC BY 4.0), NTOU's HAIDA, free NLSC orthophoto tiles and Sentinel-2 — enough for the deployment leg of the recipe on the waste side, nothing on the Raman side.

## Raman: small 1-D CNNs win, but only inside one protocol

The three papers the student cited are real and their numbers reproduce, but they cannot be ranked against each other. Liu et al. 2017 trained a pyramid-shaped 1-D CNN on two RRUFF subsets (**1,671 classes / 5,168 processed spectra** and 512 classes / 1,676 raw spectra) under a leave-one-spectrum-out split, reaching **top-1 0.884 on processed spectra vs 0.819 for a linear SVM**, and 0.933 on raw spectra where k-NN collapsed to 0.429 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.09022)). DRCNN (RSC Advances 2022) used *different* RRUFF subsets with a random stratified 70/30 split on classes having at least ten spectra and reports **0.9798 on its 192-class Dataset_1** — but its own re-implementation of Liu's CNN scores 0.9601 on that same split, which tells you most of the gap between "0.88" and "0.98" is the split, not the architecture ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8981256/fullTextXML)). Neither paper released code or weights, so the "Deep CNN" numbers in Sineesh & Kamsali's 2026 benchmark are that benchmark's re-implementation and should be cited as such ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16107v2)). The DRCNN text also has internal inconsistencies (precision quoted as accuracy, a 0.9258 figure matching no table cell), which is a useful teaching example of why a student should read tables, not abstracts ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8981256/fullTextXML)).

The legitimately comparable groups are three. Inside Sineesh & Kamsali 2026 (seven models × three datasets, one tuning grid, five runs), **SANet (2.23 M params) and the Liu-style Deep CNN (15.91 M) are the strongest**: MLROD minerals 80.34 vs 79.72 % accuracy, Bacteria-ID 30-isolate 85.62 vs 85.89 %, API pharma compounds 99.63 vs 97.02 %, while the 85 M-parameter ViT-style Transformer trails everywhere and the 4.3 M RamanFormer beats it — the authors blame untuned transformer schedules under a shared 40-epoch budget ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16107v2)). Inside DRCNN's own table (five architectures, same three splits, 30 runs each) DRCNN edges Sang et al. by 0.3–1.1 points. And inside **RamanBench (NeurIPS 2026; 77 datasets, 325,862 spectra, 28 models, one A100)** the finding is that "no single method dominates": tabular foundation models lead (TabPFN v2.5 lowest mean improvability 19.0 %, TabICL v2 26.6 %), ReZeroNet is the best non-foundation model, and time-series models such as ROCKET outrank gradient boosting on classification ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02003); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02003v4)). The practical reading for a novice project: a Liu-style or SANet 1-D CNN is a defensible "best" on every open benchmark, and a TabPFN baseline is now mandatory in any honest comparison. The one robustness number worth quoting is the MLROD distribution-shift test, where models trained on clean spectra fall from **97.8 % (Gabbro, clean) to 39.8–48.9 % on the same mineral under 50 % basaltic dust** — a within-instrument shift that cost roughly fifty points ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16107v2)).

### Training and inference cost is trivial on Colab

Every published training run in this domain fits on one consumer GPU: Liu used a single GTX-1080 for "around seven hours" over 50 epochs with shift, noise and linear-combination augmentation ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.09022)); DRCNN used a GTX Titan with batch 32, early stopping, and convergence in about 20 epochs ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8981256/fullTextXML)); the 2026 benchmark used an RTX A5000 for a 9-config grid × 40 epochs × 5 runs per model–dataset pair ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16107v2)). At the benchmark's own cost figures — **Deep CNN 21.77 M MACs per 1,024-point spectrum, RamanNet 0.72 M, Transformer 769.88 M** — the largest open training set (MLROD, 89,121 spectra) for 40 epochs is on the order of 10¹⁴ MACs, i.e. minutes on a T4; the 2017 "seven hours" reflects 2017 hardware and heavy augmentation, not an inherent cost ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16107v2)). The Colab-specific caveats are Google's, not the domain's: free-tier GPU access is "heavily restricted", limits are unpublished, and sessions cap at 12 hours ([Source](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html)). The recommended recipe grounded in the sources is Adam with cross-entropy, batch 32–512, learning rate 1e-3 to 1e-5, early stopping, shift/noise/scale augmentation, and — critically — a group-wise split that holds out an instrument or session, because the Sensors 2026 review warns that unstratified k-fold and leave-one-out "can overestimate performance" ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12788301/fullTextXML)).

Inference is a non-issue. Liu reports "less than one millisecond to process a spectrum" on the GTX-1080 (software only, excluding acquisition), DRCNN says "within milliseconds", and the benchmark's MAC counts imply a Liu-style CNN is roughly a thousand times cheaper than a ResNet-50 image forward pass ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.09022); [Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8981256/fullTextXML)). Acquisition dominates end-to-end latency — Ho et al. integrated 1–2 s per spectrum on a research microscope, and a handheld meat classifier needs "15 s per sample" mostly for acquisition — so model choice should be driven by accuracy and robustness, not speed ([Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12898-9); [Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12788301/fullTextXML)). The only deployment concern reviews raise is embedded/handheld compute, where 1-D CNNs are "difficult to deploy on portable or embedded devices" ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12788301/fullTextXML)).

### Data: 93 loadable datasets, one gated library, no general checkpoint

The student's "not many open datasets" is outdated. `pip install raman-data` (v1.7.0, October 2026, MIT) lists **93 datasets** loadable as `X, w, y = dataset.spectra, dataset.raman_shifts, dataset.targets`, and the RamanBench Hugging Face mirror holds 94 datasets in Parquet (9.62 GB, per-dataset licences, "research mirror only") ([Source](https://pypi.org/project/raman-data/); [Source](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HTW-KI-Werkstatt/RamanBench)). The catch is that most are small — RamanBench's sizes run 12 to 130,061 spectra with a **median of 235** — and licences range from CC0 and CC BY to BY-NC and Kaggle terms ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02003v4)). The everyday-materials sets the student actually wants are already in that index: `microplastics_training_*` and `microplastics_weathered` (Lei et al. 2022, 14 plastic types), `nanoplastics_individual` (five polymers), `ocean_polymers`, `synthetic_organic_pigments_*`, `organic_compounds_*`, `pharmaceutical_ingredients`, `fuel_handheld` vs `fuel_benchtop`, and the eight-instrument `bioprocess_analytes_*` set that is the only open place to run a true cross-instrument experiment ([Source](https://github.com/ml-lab-htw/raman_data)). The plastics reference libraries SLoPP (148 spectra) and SLoPP-E (113 environmentally aged) sit in an ACS Figshare collection ([Source](https://acs.figshare.com/collections/Increasing_the_Accessibility_for_Characterizing_Microplastics_Introducing_New_Application-Based_and_Spectral_Libraries_of_Plastic_Particles_SLoPP_and_SLoPP-E_/4828323)).

RRUFF itself is the weakest link in the student's plan. On 6 November 2026 both the bulk-zip directory and the download page returned a reCAPTCHA wall ("Automated traffic has been overwhelming this site"), no licence text exists on any RRUFF page, and there is no canonical train/test split — Liu, DRCNN and Soysal 2025 each defined their own ([Source](https://www.rruff.net/about/download-data/); [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25964)). Official spectrum counts (8,583 high-resolution unoriented spectra across 4,166 samples) are known only from a search snippet and are unverified. The workable path is a manual browser download followed by RamanSPy's `datasets.rruff(dataset, download=False)` loader, or the RamanLab pickle on Zenodo (CC BY 4.0, 285 MB, RRUFF + SLoPP/E + WURM) ([Source](https://ramanspy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/datasets.html); [Source](https://zenodo.org/records/15742626)). The lowest-friction classification sets for Colab are **Bacteria-ID (60,000 × 1,000 points, 30 classes, Dropbox .npy, MIT code, CC BY paper)**, the **API set (3,510 spectra, 32 compounds, CC BY, CSV)**, and MLROD (89,121 spectra, but BY-NC) ([Source](https://github.com/csho33/bacteria-ID); [Source](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Open-source_Raman_spectra_of_chemical_compounds_for_active_pharmaceutical_ingredient_development/27931131); [Source](https://ahed.nasa.gov/datasets/f5b6051bfeb18c5a7eaef6504582)). The student also misread RamanSPy: its "cancer cell lines" entries are the MDA-MB-231 denoising pairs and THP-1 volumetric scans, neither a classification dataset ([Source](https://ramanspy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/datasets.html)).

On pretrained weights the student is essentially right, with nuance. No general-purpose public checkpoint identifies arbitrary everyday chemicals. What exists is task-specific: Sineesh & Kamsali's full set of trained models (Deep CNN, SANet, RamanNet, Transformer, RamanFormer × minerals/bacteria/pharma) as a **13.9 GB CC BY 4.0 tarball on Zenodo** with MIT PyTorch code, and the Bacteria-ID ResNet checkpoints in PyTorch-0.4-era code ([Source](https://zenodo.org/records/19701494); [Source](https://github.com/asineesh/Benchmark_Raman_DeepLearning); [Source](https://github.com/csho33/bacteria-ID)). The "Raman foundation model" papers — RSPTE (IEEE Access 2026), RamanPFN (arXiv 2608.02157), DSCF — have no located weights, and the GEMS Hugging Face Space exposes a built-in encoder only through its web UI, so it is a demo, not infrastructure ([Source](https://research.google/pubs/raman-spectroscopy-pre-trained-encoder-a-self-supervised-learning-approach-for-data-efficient-domain-independent-spectroscopy-analysis/); [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02157); [Source](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JunhanCai/GEMS-Raman-Dataset)). The general tabular foundation model TabPFN, applied in-context, is per RamanBench the strongest open starting point of all ([Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02003)).

### Inputs: US$35k handhelds, US$3.5k DIY, no phone-camera chemistry

"What can we run inference on" has an uncomfortable answer for a student without a spectrometer: public spectra. Commercial handheld Raman units list at roughly **US$46,000 (Rigaku Progeny)** or "from JPY 5.6 million" (Metrohm Mira), with other vendors quote-only ([Source](https://pharma.rigaku.com/solutions/progeny-handheld-raman-1064-nm/purchase); [Source](https://pr.mono.ipros.com/en/metrohm/product/detail/2000403142)). The open-source OpenRAMAN benchtop build costs under US$3,500 at 532 nm but resolves only ~20–35 cm⁻¹, fifteen to thirty times coarser than the Horiba instrument behind Bacteria-ID ([Source](https://thestacks.org/publications/resource-diy-raman-bio); [Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12898-9)). The ~US$50 cellphone-camera Raman add-on from the Rentzepis group still needs a laser, grating and cuvette, has lower SNR than commercial units, and has no public dataset or ML component ([Source](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2023/egusphere-2023-2146/egusphere-2023-2146.pdf)). Nothing in the sources supports chemical makeup from an ordinary phone RGB camera; without a dispersive element a camera records colour and texture, so any phone-only "material" classifier is a visual object classifier, not spectroscopy. For plastic typing specifically, handheld NIR has far more field precedent: a 2025 Sensors study compared a high-resolution NIR unit with two low-cost handhelds including the open-source Plastic Scanner on six polymers with SVM/XGBoost/RF, finding the handhelds less accurate owing to limited range ([Source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12196715/)).

The student's "Raman is highly variable across instruments" is well supported and should shape the evaluation design. Reviews name inter-instrument variability a top barrier ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12788301/fullTextXML)), and the one quantitative cross-spectrometer deep-learning study, LoRA-CT (Analytical Chemistry, August 2025), shows a LoRA-transferred model (**R² 0.952**) beating both piecewise direct standardization (0.846) and full fine-tuning (0.863) with ~600× fewer trainable parameters ([Source](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.analchem.5c01846)). Ho et al. needed 100 fine-tuning spectra per isolate to go from 89.0 % to 99.0 % on clinical samples acquired under degraded optics ([Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12898-9)). The standard mitigations — interpolate to a common wavenumber grid, normalise per spectrum, augment with small shifts and noise, and fine-tune or LoRA on a small target-instrument set — are exactly the kind of cross-dataset discipline the instructor's prior project taught.

## Waste detection: dataset design, not architecture, moves the needle

### Marine debris from Sentinel-2: a Random Forest still beats the U-Net

All five marine items in the student's report exist, but two headline numbers are misread. Biermann et al. 2020 is a Gaussian naive-Bayes classifier on five hand-crafted features (the Floating Debris Index, NDVI, and three reflectances) trained on roughly **200 hand-picked pixels** (53 plastic, 48 seaweed, 60 timber, 17 spume, 20 seawater); the "86 %" is the share of *suspected*-plastic pixels at four sites that the model labelled plastic, not a held-out multi-class accuracy, and the paper reports no precision or recall ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7181820/fullTextXML)). Mifdal et al. 2021 then showed that none of Biermann's class distributions align with 157,319 in-the-wild floating-object pixels — the NB model does not transfer ([Source](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/V-3-2021/285/2021/isprs-annals-V-3-2021-285-2021.pdf)). ResAttUNet's "F1 0.95" is a *micro* F1, essentially pixel accuracy dominated by water and cloud; its macro-F1 0.77 and IoU 0.67 beat MARIDA's U-Net baseline (0.69 / 0.57) but sit **at or below MARIDA's own Random Forest with spectral indices and GLCM texture (0.79 / 0.69)**, the paper reports no Marine-Debris-class score, states no hardware or split, and remains a preprint ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.08506); [Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8740969/fullTextXML)).

The MARIDA paper itself is the clearest evidence that this is a data problem. On its scene-disjoint test split (359 patches), the Marine Debris class scores **F1 0.80 / IoU 0.67 for RF with spectral indices versus 0.50 / 0.33 for the from-scratch U-Net**, and class-average F1 is 0.79 vs 0.69 ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8740969/fullTextXML)). The reason is imbalance: Marine Debris is **3,399 of 837,357 annotated pixels (0.41 %)**, and in MADOS 4,696 of ~1.48 million (0.32 %) ([Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1765021/full); [Source](https://marine-pollution.github.io/Report_Patches_stats.html)). The student's "2023 joint-training follow-up" (Rußwurm, Jilla Venkatesa, Tuia; arXiv 2307.02465, published in iScience 26(12)) is the paper that fixed it, and it is the single most important item in the waste half: a UNet++ (~26 M params, 12-band Sen2Cor L2A, 128 px patches) trained on **FloatingObjects + MARIDA + S2Ships negatives + refined labels** reaches F1 0.749 on MARIDA's test set where the original MARIDA RF scores 0.288 and the original FloatingObjects U-Net 0.701, and F1 0.837 on Durban where the FloatingObjects U-Net collapsed to 0.497; the authors attribute the gains "mainly to dataset design (extensive negative sampling, label refinement), not architecture" ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02465v1)). Two caveats the student should carry: on MARIDA-test AUROC the merged-data RF (0.862) actually exceeds UNet++ (0.746), and the reverse direction (MARIDA-trained model on FloatingObjects' test) is never reported ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02465v1)). The weights load with `torch.hub.load("marccoru/marinedebrisdetector","unetpp")` and `pip install marinedebrisdetector`, but the **116 GB merged training set has been taken private** ("costs of hosting got too high", e-mail the author) ([Source](https://github.com/marccoru/marinedebrisdetector)).

Beyond the student's list the field moved in 2024–2026. **MADOS** (ISPRS Journal 2024; Zenodo, CC BY 4.0, 4.0 GB zip, 174 scenes, 2,803 crops of 240×240, 15 classes including Oil Spill, official split) is the natural second dataset ([Source](https://zenodo.org/records/10664073)). On its protocol the MariNeXt authors report F1 76.0 / mIoU 64.3, but the September-2026 MambaMPD paper reproduces MariNeXt at 70.6 / 59.2 and reports **MambaMPD at 74.8 / 69.8 (37 M params, 204 GFLOPs, 15 FPS on an RTX 4070 Ti, code CC0)** — quote both numbers if the student uses MariNeXt as a baseline ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15676); [Source](https://marine-pollution.github.io/)). The PANGAEA benchmark put frozen remote-sensing foundation encoders with a UPerNet head on MADOS and found **CROMA 67.55 mIoU, GFM-Swin 64.71, DOFA 59.58, a from-scratch U-Net 54.79 and Prithvi 49.98**, with TerraMind-B at 69.5 per MambaMPD's table; "GFMs trained with a wider spectral range perform best" and dropping per-band normalisation collapses Scale-MAE from 57.3 to 16.8 ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04204); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15676)). A March-2026 Frontiers paper merged MARIDA + MADOS into a binary task and reports F1 0.901 (0.881 ± 0.016 over five seeds) with cross-dataset transfer costing 1–8 F1 points (MADOS→MARIDA 0.890, MARIDA→MADOS 0.833) — though its own comparison table "mixes protocols" and its abstract contradicts its tables, so treat it as a recipe, not a leaderboard ([Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1765021/full)). The honest statement of "which results are comparable" is therefore: within MARIDA Table 4 (plus ResAttUNet's class-mean numbers), within the iScience binary protocol, within the MADOS tables (with MariNeXt's reproduced number), and within PANGAEA's frozen-encoder table — and nothing across those groups.

Training budgets range from trivial to heavy. MARIDA's U-Net is a 16-base-channel network trained for 44 epochs at batch 5 on 694 patches — minutes on a T4 ([Source](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0262247)); the Frontiers ~31 M-parameter binary U-Net took **195 minutes on an RTX 4080** for 100 epochs on 3,291 patches with BCE+Dice+focal loss ([Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1765021/full)); the iScience UNet++ took **≈8 hours on one RTX 3090** over the now-private 116 GB corpus ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02465v1)). Inference is fast: the iScience CLI processes a full Durban scene (3,122 × 3,843 px, ~300 MB) in **11 s on an RTX 3090, 15 s on a GTX 970 and 11 minutes on a MacBook CPU**, the Frontiers U-Net runs 22 tiles/s on an RTX 4080, and a full 110 km Sentinel-2 tile is about eight Durban scenes, so minutes per tile on a Colab GPU ([Source](https://github.com/marccoru/marinedebrisdetector); [Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1765021/full)). One preprocessing trap matters for any combination: MARIDA and MADOS are 11-band ACOLITE Rayleigh-corrected, the iScience model expects 12-band Sen2Cor L2A, and FloatingObjects mixes L1C/L2A; TOA vs BOA alone changed the iScience Durban detections from 164 to 136 debris objects ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02465v1)).

### Land waste: Swin-T at 20 cm is the number to beat, and "90 %" is a press release

AerialWaste has two versions and the student should say which one. Version 1 (Scientific Data, January 2023) is **10,434 RGB tiles of Lombardy (3,478 positive / 6,956 negative)**, each a 210 × 210 m square from AGEA airborne orthophotos (~20 cm), WorldView-3 (~30 cm) or Google Earth (~50 cm), with binary labels, a 715-image multi-label subset (22 labels), 841 masks on 169 test images, and no georeferencing; the published baseline is a ResNet50+FPN binary classifier at **AP 87.99 % / F1 80.70 %** ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML)). Version 3 on Zenodo (July 2024, modified February 2026) has 11,703 tiles in 18.8 GB and switched the licence from CC BY to **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** — fine for coursework, but it forbids redistributing a re-split or re-labelled copy ([Source](https://zenodo.org/records/12607190); [Source](https://zenodo.org/records/7034382)). The best peer-reviewed number on v3 is the Polimi/PERIVALLON pipeline paper (Gibellini et al. 2025, Waste Management Bulletin): across 36 configurations, **Swin-T with Million-AID remote-sensing pretraining at 20 cm and 100 m context (500 px) scores F1 92.02 % / accuracy 94.56 %**, the best ResNet-50 scores 90.98 %, Swin-T wins every cell, GSD has a "negligible" effect between 20 and 50 cm, and remote-sensing pretraining beats ImageNet by 1–3 F1 points ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)). The same paper is the only real-world generalisation evidence: the Lombardy model loses on average **5.1 F1 points on Greece, Sweden and Romania** (recall 77–87 %, precision ≥ 90 %), and in a field study with four ARPA Lombardia professionals the AI-assisted workflow found 155 sites versus 95 manually while inspecting 60 % less area ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)).

The student's "lightweight AerialWaste classifiers" item (arXiv 2508.18315) does not support the inference drawn from it. The paper trained on a free Colab GPU at 224 px on its own re-split of v3 with **manually edited labels**, and reports MobileNetV2_050 with ten frozen layers at accuracy 90.68 / F1 90.69, MobileViT-XS at 91.25 / 91.37, and a three-transformer probability fusion at weighted F1 92.41 — but the positive-class F1 of that best model is 88.99, no heavy model is evaluated at all (the "deepest" tested are DenseNet121 and GoogLeNet), no train/validation gap is shown, batch size is stated as both 16 and 64, and no timing, FLOPs or parameter counts appear despite "computational efficiency" being a listed contribution ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)). So "lightweight models overfit less" is an assertion in that paper's abstract, not a result, and Gibellini's 27 M-parameter Swin-T beating ResNet-50 on the same data points the other way. The defensible claim is that ImageNet-pretrained small backbones at 224 px reach ~89–91 % accuracy on a 2:1 negative-heavy split, within ~3 points of the best 500-px Swin-T, at a fraction of the compute (MobileNet_V2 is 0.3 GFLOPs at 224 px versus Swin_T's 4.49) ([Source](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/stable/models.html)). Because the test split is two-thirds negatives, a constant "no waste" classifier scores ~66.7 % accuracy; F1 or AP on the positive class is the honest metric.

PERIVALLON's "over 90 % accuracy" is a Politecnico di Milano news item (21 July 2025) with no metric definition, test set, paper citation or sensor named; the CORDIS project page lists no waste-detection deliverables, and the project closed in November 2025 ([Source](https://www.polimi.it/en/research/news/detail/ai-and-satellites-to-detect-illegal-landfills-with-90-accuracy); [Source](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101073952)). The most plausible referent is Gibellini's 94.56 % Lombardy accuracy — which, as above, is flattered by the split. The comparable groups on land are: all 36 cells inside Gibellini 2025; the tables inside 2508.18315 (on their own re-split); Torres 2023 versus Molina 2024 on the v1 split, which differ in input size (800 vs 256 px) and metric; and nothing across those papers. Molina 2024 is also the resolution-sensitivity study the instructor will want: a ResNet-50 at 256 px reaches F1 0.86, still 0.79 at 32 px (~6–16 m effective GSD), **0.43 at 8 px, and a 256-px model applied to 32-px inputs collapses to 0.20** ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01790v1)). That is why Sentinel-2 at 10 m is the wrong sensor for landfill-scale targets: the one 2026 Sentinel-2 landfill study reaches only **54 % AP** for its YOLO landfill agent and needs road and industry context to reach a usable posterior ([Source](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/XI-3-2026/849/2026/isprs-annals-XI-3-2026-849-2026.pdf)).

Training compute on land is as modest as at sea: the 2021 baseline used two RTX 2080 Ti (batch 12, lr 0.005, BCE, early stopping) ([Source](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-04520/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-04520.pdf)); Gibellini ran inference over 100 km² on one RTX 2080 Ti in time "negligible" relative to human inspection ([Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)); and Sun et al.'s Nature Communications global dumpsite detector (BCA-Net, Faster R-CNN + FPN; sensitivity 0.98 but precision 0.70) trained on eight RTX 3090s "within a few hours" and scans 162 km² of 0.3–1 m imagery in under 30 seconds on a laptop ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10015540/fullTextXML)). No paper reports wall-clock training time for any AerialWaste model; 2508.18315 is the existence proof that full v3 trains on free Colab at 224 px. For litter-scale objects the open sets are drone and phone imagery: UAVVaste (772 images, CC BY 4.0, 3.0 GB; YOLOv4 mAP@50 0.785, with INT8 quantisation destroying accuracy and Jetson Xavier NX throughput of 5.4 FPS FP32 / 19.3 FPS FP16 for YOLOv4), TACO (1,500 images, 60 classes, CC BY 4.0; Mask R-CNN AP only 26.2 classless), and the Polimi/CERTH **DroneWaste (September 2025; CC BY 4.0, 4,993 tiles at 2 cm/px, 20 European-Waste-Code materials, masks + boxes; YOLOv8x mAP@50 38.2 %)** — the last being the only land dataset whose labels are *materials*, which matters for the combination question ([Source](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-00965/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-00965.pdf); [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06975); [Source](https://zenodo.org/records/17045559); [Source](https://github.com/lucamora/dronewaste)).

### Free imagery, and the physics of what each sensor can see

Sentinel-2 is free everywhere: the Copernicus Data Space gives a general user 12 TB per month of transfer plus 10,000 Sentinel Hub processing units and 10,000 openEO credits ([Source](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/Quotas.html)); Google Earth Engine serves `COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED` from March 2017 to the present, free for research and education ([Source](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_S2_SR_HARMONIZED)); Microsoft Planetary Computer serves L2A COGs from June 2015 with anonymous SAS tokens ([Source](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/api/stac/v1/collections/sentinel-2-l2a)). EU law grants "free, full and open access" for reproduction, adaptation and combination with attribution ([Source](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/documents/247904/690755/Sentinel_Data_Legal_Notice)). The constellation flew three satellites from March 2025 to March 2026 after Sentinel-2C's launch; the normal revisit is five days ([Source](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/Data/SentinelMissions/Sentinel2.html)). What it cannot see is individual items: the empirical detection floor is **about 20 % plastic cover of a 10 × 10 m pixel** (PLP target campaigns; Cózar 2024's Mediterranean windrow catalogue of 14,374 detections used filaments longer than 70 m), Biermann's targets were 30–55 % sub-pixel, and the Frontiers authors put the reliable threshold at aggregations above ~100 m² ([Source](https://nebula.esa.int/sites/default/files/neb_tec_study/1459/public/C4000131040ExS.pdf); [Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11178853/fullTextXML); [Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7181820/fullTextXML)). This is why every benchmark is built from reported events at known hotspots, and why the literature's remedy is negative mining over ordinary ocean (S2Ships, random background, proxy-classifier false positives) rather than a change of sensor; the student's "non-targeted locations need commercial high-resolution imagery" is not what the papers say ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02465v1)).

For land targets the trained models need ≤ 50 cm imagery, but that need not be commercial. NAIP covers the US at 30–100 cm in the public domain ([Source](https://registry.opendata.aws/naip/)); Spain's PNOA is at 10 cm in Earth Engine, "free use for any legitimate purpose" with IGN attribution ([Source](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/Spain_PNOA_PNOA10)); Estonia's national orthophotos are "for everybody to use free of charge" ([Source](https://geoportaal.maaruum.ee/eng/spatial-data/orthophotos/download-orthophotos-p662.html)); Dutch, Latvian, Swiss, Luxembourg and some German state orthophotos appear open but were verified only from snippets. The trap is Google: the Maps Platform terms forbid bulk tile download and explicitly list using Google Maps content "to improve machine learning and artificial intelligence models, including to train, test, validate or fine-tune the models" as prohibited ([Source](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms)), yet AerialWaste ships Google Earth tiles "subject to Google's terms" and Gibellini's Swedish and Romanian test sets were built from Google Earth. A student should train and evaluate on the AGEA/WorldView tiles where possible and never republish Google-sourced derivatives. The usable pretrained aerial backbones are in TorchGeo — SatLAS NAIP Swin-V2-B (ODC-BY) is the closest free match to 20–50 cm tiles, Clay v1.5 is Apache-2.0, and Prithvi-EO-2.0 (30 m HLS, six bands) is the wrong scale for either task ([Source](https://docs.torchgeo.org/en/stable/api/models.html); [Source](https://clay-foundation.github.io/model/index.html); [Source](https://huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial/Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M)).

## Taiwan supplies evaluation targets, not training labels

Taiwan has no pixel- or polygon-level label set for Sentinel-2 floating debris, no released illegal-dump imagery with labels, and no open Raman or NIR spectral dataset at all. What it has — and this is enough for the deployment leg of the instructor's recipe on the waste side — is coarse but real ground truth, which is exactly how the COA shelter data functioned in the prior project. The Society of Wilderness (SOW) and Greenpeace rapid-screening survey divided the ~1,210 km main-island coastline into **121 stations ~10 km apart**, surveyed them quarterly in 2018–19, and converted volumes to 90 L bag counts (≈12.27 million litres / 646 tonnes extrapolated; 56 % of debris on 10 % of the coastline) — but the per-station table is not online and must be requested from SOW ([Source](https://www.greenpeace.org/taiwan/press/7747/); [Source](https://www.greenpeace.org/taiwan/update/12782/第二次快篩結果，限塑淨灘有感/)). The 2022 re-survey of the 14 dirtiest segments *is* downloadable: SOW's four-page press-release PDF reproduces the full per-site table of average bags per 100 m in 2018 and 2022 (e.g. 新北市瑞芳區 144.8 → 119.5; 雲林縣麥寮鄉 62.5 → 71.25; 屏東縣滿州鄉 33.9 → 47.25) ([Source](https://www.sow.org.tw/sites/sow/files/xin_wen_gao__huang_ye_qi_dong_hai_fei_fu_cha__jian_shi_tai_wan_hai_fei__fa_xian_bu_fen_re_dian_bu_jian_fan_zeng_.pdf)). A Sentinel-2 model's per-segment debris score rank-correlated (Spearman) against those bags-per-100 m values is the direct analogue of the Spearman-vs-published-city-rates evaluation in the prior project. The Ocean Conservation Administration publishes **monthly county-level cleanup statistics (≈85 monthly CSV/JSON files, ROC 108.06 → 115.06)** on data.gov.tw under the Open Government Data Licence v1, with tonnes by source and by item type (PET bottles, cans, glass, foam, fishing gear) — too coarse for pixels, but a sanity-check time series for county-aggregated detections ([Source](https://data.gov.tw/dataset/172912)).

The government's own drone/AI programme is the closest thing to an AerialWaste-style Taiwan dataset, and it is not released. The Environmental Management Administration's 向海致敬 surveys flew over 178 inspection flights across more than 400 km of coast in 2020–2025, report AI-versus-manual agreement above 81 %, and plan resident automated drone patrols for the second half of 2026 ([Source](https://udn.com/news/story/7266/9550631)); a December-2025 article in 《工程》 by EMA and vendor 環輿科技 describes a YOLOv4→YOLOv9 model with a training set grown from ~495 to 14,489 images and four-level beach-coverage grades, but the PDF's custom font made those figures only partially legible and they should be treated as unverified ([Source](https://www.cie.org.tw/var/file/0/1000/img/2/708585316.pdf)). Two Taiwan image datasets are downloadable. **MDImageNet** (國家海洋研究院, released 30 July 2026, **CC BY 4.0**) holds over 20,000 real coastal images with over 42,000 YOLO bounding boxes in 34 ICC-based classes mapped to 20 scoring classes (plastic bottles, caps, straws, bags, cans, cigarette butts, fishing nets, floats, syringes, toothbrushes, …), was the basis of an AIdea competition with mAP@0.5 scoring, and ships a NAMR baseline model — though the site is a JavaScript app and the download mechanics could not be confirmed ([Source](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260730701780-431204); [Source](https://www.digitimes.com.tw/seminar/mdivrc_2026/index.html)). NTOU's **HAIDA/HAIDA2** high-resolution aerial trash dataset is on GitHub under MIT with Google Drive links and a companion Aerospace 2023 paper (YOLOv3/v4/Scaled-v4 with k-fold CV), but the README omits image count, classes and GSD ([Source](https://github.com/LiaoSteve/HAIDA-Trash-Dataset-High-Resolution-Aerial-image); [Source](https://scholars.ntou.edu.tw/handle/123456789/23732)). Both are ground/low-altitude, not satellite; they support a drone-scale branch and, because their classes are ICC item types, align directly with the SOW/OCA item categories.

For land dumping, the best analogue to "government open data as evaluation labels" is the Ministry of the Interior's 國土利用監測 land-use change detection, run by NCU's remote-sensing centre on SPOT imagery since 2018 and flagging "變異點" (variation points) in 64 types, three of which are waste-related; The Reporter downloaded the raw 2008–2022 variation-point file in 2022 and found **132,254 points, 4,748 confirmed waste/soil-dumping violations, and 770 points within 3 km of the 台61 coastal highway** ([Source](https://www.twreporter.org/a/enterprise-wastes-black-market-satellite-images-find-abandoned-dirt-and-waste)). A type-coded, dated point file with verification outcomes is structurally the same label as the COA data was; an AerialWaste-trained classifier applied to orthophoto chips at those coordinates could be scored on confirmed versus rejected points. The imagery for those chips is free: NLSC's WMTS endpoint serves orthophoto layers (PHOTO2, PHOTO2014…PHOTO2020) in EPSG:3857 without an application, though the capabilities XML carries no licence text and the OSM community has treated NLSC data as non-open, so redistribution needs a licence check ([Source](https://wmts.nlsc.gov.tw/wmts?SERVICE=WMTS&REQUEST=GetCapabilities)). FORMOSAT-2/5 scenes are free to domestic academia in limited quantity after an application through the NSTC data market, and FORMOSAT-8 (launched 29 November 2025, 1 m native / 0.7 m super-resolved, with a red-edge band) has no published academic channel yet ([Source](https://data.gov.tw/dataset/146051); [Source](https://technews.tw/2026/10/15/formosat-8-satellite-taiwan/)). No data.gov.tw dataset ID for the variation points was found; access is through the 國土利用監測整合資訊網 and needs a direct check.

On the chemistry side Taiwan offers nothing open. The only Taiwan-origin Raman data are NCHU Big Data Center SERS sets (pesticides, Sudan dyes) on the NCHC platform, already split into train/valid/test CSVs but **"Application Required"** with zero downloads ([Source](https://ark.nchc.org.tw/en/dataset/smarter_smds_sers01_0_sers_20220902_spec_000002)); NCHU's VNIR/NIR rice-disease hyperspectral sets need an authorisation agreement; and the OCA publishes an open CSV of FTIR polymer-identification *results* (match score, polymer name, station, season) for 2021–2023 microplastic samples — a Taiwan polymer-class prior, not spectra ([Source](https://data.gov.tw/dataset/172479)). After searching in English and Traditional Chinese across Zenodo, Figshare, Kaggle, GitHub and NCHC, the explicit finding is that no openly downloadable Taiwan Raman or NIR library exists as of 6 November 2026, and no Taiwan Sentinel-2 marine-debris paper, deep-learning landfill paper, or Raman-ML paper with released data was found either. Taiwan's academic litter work is UAV/ground YOLO at NTOU, NCU and NCKU, of which only HAIDA has public data ([Source](https://scholars.ntou.edu.tw/handle/123456789/23732)).

### Why Raman-plus-satellite is a category error

The physics closes the door on a fused trunk. Plastic features in VNIR/SWIR reflectance are broad, overlapping overtone and combination bands of C–H vibrations (PET near 1660 nm, PE near 1210 and 1430 nm, PP near 1190 and 1400 nm), with black plastics excluded outright for "very low reflectance in the NIR" ([Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6747759/fullTextXML)); Raman peaks are sharp fundamentals on a cm⁻¹ shift axis. Of the eight absorption features Garaba & Dierssen measured on marine-harvested plastics, only **~1215 and ~1732 nm are usable from orbit**, because the 931 and 1417 nm features fall in water-vapour windows ([Source](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/12/77/2020/)). Sentinel-2's SWIR bands sit at 1610 and 2190 nm — neither — so its FDI/PI indices detect "floating anomaly", not chemistry ([Source](https://s2.pages.eopf.copernicus.eu/pdfs-adfs/MSI/L0/PDFS_S2_MSI_L0plus.myst.html)). Water removes the signal entirely: a submerged place mat at 8 cm depth showed "no measurable reflectance beyond 1134 nm" ([Source](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/13/713/2021/)), and Hu 2022 warns that sub-pixel debris (<10 % of a pixel) so distorts MSI spectra that linking them to specific materials is unsafe ([Source](https://ioccg.org/bibliography/hu-c-2022-rsmld-3/)). The one successful cross-modal vibrational model, Vib2Mol, fuses Raman and IR as channels precisely because both span 400–4000 cm⁻¹, and it excludes NIR reflectance ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07014v4)). No paper attempts Raman → reflectance transfer; none was found after searching. What *can* transfer between the two tasks is the label space (polymer type) and the design pattern (1-D spectral encoder + classifier head) — not weights, and not a shared representation.

The same logic disposes of the student's "related" STARCOP/MethaneMapper items. Both are verified and downloadable, and both work the same way: a physics-derived matched-filter product (mag1c / the HITRAN CH₄ signature) is computed per pixel from hundreds of AVIRIS-NG bands, then a small 2-D network segments the spatial plume — HyperSTARCOP is a ~6.6 M-parameter MobileNetV2-U-Net trained on one V100 for 6–8 hours, and its "sensor-agnostic" zero-shot transfer to EMIT works because the input is the filter output, not raw bands ([Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44918-6); [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.02767)). The "chemical" is baked into the filter, so the methane weights do not transfer to plastics. The recipe does: Estrela et al. 2025 ran a matched filter against a plastic reflectance library on EMIT's 60 m, 380–2500 nm data and identified **HDPE and PVC signatures across multiple continents**, mostly agricultural ([Source](https://earth.jpl.nasa.gov/emit/applications/application-areas/plastics/)). That is the right template for a satellite chemistry head, and it requires EMIT (open, unrestricted), EnMAP (free with institutional registration) or PRISMA (free, non-redistributable) — not Sentinel-2 ([Source](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-emitl2bmin-001); [Source](https://geoservice.dlr.de/resources/licenses/enmap/EnMAP-Data_License.pdf); [Source](https://www.asi.it/en/?p=17373)).

### A Colab feasibility ranking for one novice student

Ranked by cost and honesty, the options are as follows. The cheapest real combination is the two-stage pipeline: run the iScience UNet++ zero-shot via Torch Hub on free Sentinel-2 L2A over Taiwan hotspots, aggregate per SOW segment, and separately train a Liu-style 1-D CNN on MADLib (polymer labels, 210 MB) with Knaeps' wet/submerged spectra as a robustness test — every input is under 1 GB except the imagery, and the design is explicit that the chemistry model never sees a satellite pixel. Next is the shared-trunk analogue of the instructor's recipe: a frozen TorchGeo encoder (CROMA or DOFA, both benchmarked on MADOS) or a small U-Net with a MARIDA head, a MADOS head and an auxiliary index/material head, trained on 11-band patches with class-weighted or BCE+Dice+focal loss at 128–256 px for ≤100 epochs — well under the 195 minutes the Frontiers model needed on an RTX 4080, and Prithvi-EO-2.0's Colab-T4 fine-tuning notebooks show the tooling works on free hardware ([Source](https://huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial/Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M)). Third, a STARCOP-style U-Net retrained on a plastic matched-filter product using the HF mini set and Colab notebooks is feasible but requires the student to build the filter from a MADLib signature convolved to the sensor response ([Source](https://github.com/spaceml-org/STARCOP)). Last, anything on EnMAP/PRISMA/EMIT with HyperSIGMA or DOFA at hyperspectral resolution is a research project: 30–60 m pixels, no polymer-labelled scene set, and HyperSIGMA was pretrained on 64–128 accelerators ([Source](https://github.com/WHU-Sigma/HyperSIGMA)). The direct answer to question six is therefore no, not as one model — but yes as a shared label space over a pipeline, and yes as a within-modality multi-task Sentinel-2 model whose auxiliary head is a spectral quantity rather than a Raman output; the literature contains no quantitative test of how much such a material head helps MARIDA segmentation, which makes it a small, defensible, novel student project rather than a replication.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the student's project from "combine Raman with satellites" to "share a materials vocabulary across a detector and a classifier," and it shows that the instructor's own cross-dataset recipe has a clean, literature-grounded analogue only on the waste side: Sentinel-2 is the one modality where two open foreign datasets (MARIDA, MADOS) with the same preprocessing, a multi-dataset precedent with public weights (Rußwurm 2023), a shared-trunk precedent (Inzerillo 2025), free global imagery, and a Taiwan government/NGO evaluation target (SOW bags-per-100 m, OCA tonnage) all line up. The Raman side has better benchmarks, cheaper compute and more open data than the student believed, but no Taiwan leg at all, so it can only enter the project as the second stage of a pipeline or as a ground-truthing instrument the student does not own.

Two implications follow for planning. First, the auxiliary head is where the pedagogical novelty lives: no paper has measured whether a spectral or material auxiliary target helps MARIDA/MADOS debris segmentation, so a novice can produce a genuinely new (if small) result with a few hundred megabytes of data and a T4. Second, the discipline the prior project taught — scene-disjoint splits, positive-class metrics on imbalanced data, held-out instruments or regions, and distrust of headline numbers that mix protocols — is exactly what this literature rewards, because in both domains the published gains came from how the data were split, negatives sampled and labels refined, not from the architecture.
