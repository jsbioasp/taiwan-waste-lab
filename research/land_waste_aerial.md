# Deep-learning detection of land-based waste (landfills, illegal dumps, litter) from satellite, aerial and drone imagery — state as of 6 Nov 2026

Scope: land waste only. Marine debris (MARIDA, MADOS, PlastOPol, etc.) and Taiwan-specific data are excluded; no Taiwan-specific land-waste item was encountered during this research. All dates are publication dates. Items marked **[unverified]** come from secondary sources or search snippets I could not open; items marked **[inference]** are my own arithmetic or reasoning, not a sourced claim.

## KQ1. AerialWaste: exact composition, annotations, access, license, baselines, and the follow-up papers (arXiv 2508.18315, PERIVALLON "90%")

### Takeaway
AerialWaste v1 (Scientific Data, 31 Jan 2023) is 10,434 RGB tiles (3,478 positive / 6,956 negative) of Lombardy, Italy, each a 210 × 210 m square from AGEA airborne orthophotos (~20 cm GSD, ~1000 px), WorldView-3 (~30 cm, ~700 px) and Google Earth (~50 cm, ~1000 px), with image-level binary labels, a 715-image multi-label subset (22 labels) and 841 masks on 169 test images; the current Zenodo v3 (30 Jul 2024) has ~11,700 tiles (7,278 train / 2,607 test as used by 2508.18315) under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. The published baseline is a ResNet50+FPN binary classifier (AP 87.99 %, F1 80.70 %); the "90 %" PERIVALLON figure is a press-release number with no stated metric or test set, and the closest peer-reviewed candidate is Gibellini et al. 2025 (Swin-T: 94.56 % accuracy / 92.02 % F1 on the v3 Lombardy test split).

### Cited Findings
**Composition (v1, Scientific Data paper)**
- Published 31 January 2023, Scientific Data 10:63 (received 6 Sep 2022). Total 10,434 images: 3,478 positive, 6,956 negative. Sources: AGEA orthophotos (airborne RGB, ≈20 cm GSD, ≈1000 × 1000 px), WorldView-3 (pan-sharpened RGB, ≈30 cm GSD, ≈700 × 700 px), Google Earth (≈50 cm GSD, ≈1000 × 1000 px, upsampled ×2 by the API). Each image is a 210 × 210 m square centred on a location. Per-source counts are given only graphically (Fig. 4): Google Earth contributes the most, then AGEA, then WorldView-3. — [Torres & Fraternali 2023, full text via Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML); DOI [10.1038/s41597-023-01976-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-01976-9)
- Split: 75 % training / 25 % test (exact counts not stated in text). — [same](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML)
- Annotation granularities: (1) binary waste present/absent; (2) multi-label metadata on 715 samples across 22 labels = 15 object types + 7 storage modes, 2,103 label assignments total. Object types (count): rubble/excavated earth and rocks 294; bulky items 286; fire wood 173; scrap 167; plastic 126; vehicles 53; tires 45; paper 26; domestic appliances 24; sludge/zootechnical waste/manure 19; stone/marble processing waste 13; asphalt milling 12; corrugated sheets presumed asbestos-cement 11; foundry waste 9; glass 8. Storage modes: heaps not delimited 448; containers 167; delimited heaps 69; big bags 50; pallets 50; cisterns 35; drums/bins 18. (3) Segmentation masks: 169 test images, 841 masks over 9 classes, drawn with the ODIN tool, intended for weakly-supervised-localization evaluation only (no localization metric reported). Positive sites also carry evidence (0–3) and severity (0–3) scales, area type and storage mode (severity/area type present for ~72 %). — [same](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML)
- Annotation/sampling process: four territory-monitoring professionals at ARPA Lombardia built a location database of 10,434 locations across 487 municipalities (~33 % analyst-reported positives). Negatives: two random locations per positive, placed 1–5 km away to keep similar land use. The location database is withheld "for confidentiality reasons" and images are **not geo-referenced**. — [same](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML)
- Format: MS-COCO-style JSON (training.json / testing.json) plus image folders. — [same](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML); [aerialwaste.org](https://aerialwaste.org/)

**Published baseline (v1) and released weights**
- Baseline: binary CNN, ResNet50 backbone with FPN links, ImageNet transfer learning, first two layers frozen, inputs resized to a fixed size, normalized by dataset mean/std, flip/rotation/crop augmentation, sigmoid output thresholded at 0.5. Test set (all three sources): **AP 87.99 %, F1 80.70 %, precision 81.89 %, recall 79.54 %**. Accuracy and AUC not reported; per-source F1 only in Fig. 10. Model trained/tested on AGEA only: AP 94.5 %, F1 88.2 % (from the 2021 paper). Field validation: analysts scanning new AGEA imagery found 69 sites, of which 65 were also flagged by the CNN; AGEA-only model flagged all 69. — [Torres & Fraternali 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML)
- Released model repo: ResNet50 + FPN, sigmoid output, "all images are resized to 800 × 800 during training", lr 0.005, batch 12, BCE loss, early stopping patience 10 / min delta 0.0005; weights as `checkpoint.pth` on a Google Drive folder; notebook `execute_model.ipynb` outputs score + CAMs; license CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; no metrics in README. — [rnt-pmi/aerialwaste-model](https://github.com/rnt-pmi/aerialwaste-model)
- The 2021 precursor (Torres & Fraternali, Remote Sensing 13(22):4520, 10 Nov 2021): ≈3,000 AGEA images at 20 cm/px (990 positives from ARPA over 105 municipalities, ≈2,000 negatives), geographic split 75/13/12 %, image sizes randomly 600 px (120 m) / 800 px (160 m) / 1000 px (200 m), ResNet50+FPN, lr 0.005, batch 12, trained on **two NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti**, test: AP 94.5 %, accuracy 92.6 %, F1 88.2 %, precision 88.6 %, recall 87.7 %, ECE 7.01 at threshold 0.44; weights at github.com/rnt-pmi/remote-sensing-scene-classification-landfills. — [Torres & Fraternali 2021 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-04520/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-04520.pdf); DOI [10.3390/rs13224520](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13224520)

**arXiv 2508.18315 (Sharmily et al., submitted 23 Aug 2025; IEEE Access template; Dhaka / Qatar University)**
- Data handling: AerialWaste v3 (11,703 images); 7,278 train → 1,818 validation carved out (20 %), 2,607 test. All images resized to 256 × 256 (preprocessing) and then "resized to 224 × 224 before feeding"; positives augmented to balance train folders at 4,852 each; **they manually re-labelled some training images they judged wrong** (Figs. 2–3). Normalization mean [0.3201, 0.3334, 0.2832], std [0.2004, 0.1818, 0.1764]. — [arXiv 2508.18315 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)

No inference time, FLOPs or parameter counts are reported anywhere in the paper. — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)
- Table III (single CNNs, test set, %): DenseNet121 acc 89.14 / F1 89.20; MobileNetV2_050 89.80 / 89.86; GoogLeNet 88.34 / 88.14; SqueezeNet1_0 89.34 / 89.47; MobileNetV2_050 with 10 frozen layers **90.68 / 90.69**. Table IV: MobileViT-XS 91.25 / 91.37; ViT-Tiny (vit_tiny_r_s16_p8_224) 90.95 / 90.98; Parallel ensemble (concatenated features + FC) **91.56 / 91.59**, AUC 0.97. Table V (probability averaging of the three transformer models): weighted acc 92.33, precision 92.67, sensitivity 92.33, F1 92.41, specificity 92.71 — but **positive-class F1 is 88.99 (precision 85.23, recall 93.09)** and negative-class F1 94.11. Optimizer ablation: AdamW best (acc 91.14). — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)
- The "overfitting" claim is stated qualitatively ("we found complex and heavy models to be prone to overfitting"); **no train/validation gap or any result for a heavy model is reported** — the heaviest tested are DenseNet121 (8.0 M params) and GoogLeNet. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18315); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)
- Their Table VII compares against Torres 2023 (AP 87.99 / F1 80.70) and Molina et al. 2024 (acc 0.9044 / F1 0.8600) while using a different split, different image size and edited labels. — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)

**Gibellini et al. 2025 — the Polimi/PERIVALLON pipeline paper**
- arXiv 2502.06607 v1 "Illegal Waste Detection in Remote Sensing Images: A Case Study" (10 Feb 2025); v4 (17 Sep 2025) retitled "A Deep Learning Pipeline for Solid Waste Detection in Remote Sensing Images", Waste Management Bulletin 3 (2025) 100246, DOI 10.1016/j.wmb.2025.100246. — [arXiv listing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06607); [PDF v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)

Best: **Swin-T, RSP, 20 cm, 100 m context (500 px): F1 92.02 %, precision 90.02 %, recall 94.13 %, accuracy 94.56 %**. Best ResNet-50: 30 cm / 100 m / RSP, F1 90.98 %, acc 94.02 %. Swin-T beats ResNet-50 in every cell; GSD effect "negligible" in 20–50 cm; RSP > INP by ~1–3 F1 points. — [PDF v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)
- Generalization (100 × 100 m tiles): Greece (WorldView-3, 2 Jul 2024; 116 pos / 137 neg) F1 85.45 %, P 90.38 %, R 81.03 %, acc 87.35 %; Sweden (Google Earth; 187/193) F1 83.82 %, R 77.54 %; Romania (Google Earth; 185/133) F1 91.48 %, R 87.03 %. Average acc 87.73 %, F1 86.92 % = −6.83 / −5.10 points vs Lombardy. — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)
- Utility study with 4 ARPA Lombardia professionals over 12 municipalities: AI-assisted (score > 0.2) found 155 sites vs 95 manual while inspecting 60.2 % less area (49.84 vs 125.24 km²); per-site time −12.2 %; with threshold 0.7, ~30 % total time reduction. — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** The "over 90 %" press figure most plausibly refers to the Gibellini et al. 2025 Lombardy test result (94.56 % accuracy / 92.02 % F1 on a 2:1 negative-heavy test split), not to the generalization sets (average accuracy 87.73 %) nor to the 2023 baseline (F1 80.70 %). Because the test split is 2/3 negatives, a trivial "always negative" classifier scores ≈66.7 % accuracy, so accuracy overstates discriminative power; F1 or AP on the positive class is the honest number.
- **[inference]** The student's statement that lightweight models "overfit less than heavier models" is not supported by evidence inside 2508.18315: no heavy model result is reported and the "deeper" models tested (DenseNet121, GoogLeNet) are themselves small. Meanwhile Gibellini et al. show a 27 M-parameter Swin-T beating ResNet-50 on the same data, and ensembles of transformers were the best in 2508.18315 itself. The defensible claim is "on AerialWaste, ImageNet-pretrained small backbones at 224 px reach ~89–91 % accuracy, within ~3 points of the best published 500-px Swin-T".
- **[inference]** v1 → v3 grew from 10,434 to 11,703 tiles; any paper must state which version and which split it used before results are compared.

### Gaps
- Per-source counts and per-source F1 for AerialWaste are only in figures (Fig. 4 and Fig. 10 of the 2023 paper); I could not read them numerically.
- No peer-reviewed document defines the PERIVALLON "90 %" metric; project deliverables are not on CORDIS.
- 2508.18315 reports no parameter counts, FLOPs, timings, GPU model, random seeds or confidence intervals, and its batch size is stated inconsistently (16 vs 64); no code/weights link was found.

## KQ2. Which models perform best, and which comparisons are legitimate (same dataset / split / metric)?

### Takeaway
On landfill-scale scene classification the best published number on AerialWaste v3 is Swin-T (Million-AID pretrained, 20 cm, 100 m context): F1 92.02 % / acc 94.56 %; on v1 the official ResNet50+FPN baseline is AP 87.99 % / F1 80.70 %. For litter-scale detection, full-size YOLOv4 (mAP@50 0.785) and EfficientDet-d3 (0.751) lead on UAVVaste; Mask R-CNN on TACO gives only AP 26.2 (TACO-1) / 19.4 (TACO-10). Across papers, only a few comparisons are apples-to-apples; most differ in dataset version, split, input size, label edits or metric.

### Inferences
- **Legitimately comparable (same data + split + metric):**
  (a) Inside Gibellini 2025: all 36 configs (ResNet-50 vs Swin-T, GSD, context, pretraining) — same v3 split and threshold.
  (b) Inside 2508.18315: Tables III–VI (all on their own re-split, re-labelled, 224-px version of v3).
  (c) Inside UAVVaste Table 2: nine detectors at 608 px on the same split.
  (d) Inside Majchrowska 2021 Table 4 (four detectors, detect-waste one-class).
  (e) Inside DroneWaste: three detectors, same 17-fold protocol.
  (f) Torres 2023 (v1 split, ResNet50+FPN) vs Molina 2024 "original 256 px" (same v1 split): comparable in spirit but the metric differs (AP/F1 at 0.5 vs F1 at tuned threshold) and input size differs (800 vs 256 px).
- **Not comparable:** 2508.18315 vs Gibellini 2025 (different split carve-out, label edits, 224 vs 500 px, accuracy on a 2:1 test); 2508.18315 vs Torres 2023 (v3 vs v1, AP vs accuracy); UAVVaste mAP@50 (boxes, 608 px) vs TACO AP (masks, averaged IoU 0.5–0.95, 1024 px); EfficientDet-D2 74.1 on UAVVaste (Majchrowska) vs YOLOv4 0.785 (Kraft) — same dataset but different input size, training recipe and possibly split; Sun 2023 sensitivity/precision at detection level vs scene-classification F1.
- **[inference]** Ranking by evidence weight for a Colab student: for AerialWaste-style scene classification, a Swin-T/ConvNeXt-T or ResNet-50 with remote-sensing pretraining at ≥ 332–500 px is the strongest documented recipe; MobileNetV2/EfficientNet-B0 at 224 px costs ~2–4 F1 points but trains in a fraction of the time. For litter in drone imagery, a YOLOv8-s/m or EfficientDet-D1/D2 at 608–1024 px is the documented sweet spot; Mask R-CNN is consistently the weakest of the modern detectors on these datasets.

### Gaps
- No paper reports confidence intervals or multiple seeds for AerialWaste classification; differences of 1–2 points between lightweight models (Table III of 2508.18315) are likely within noise. **[inference]**
- I could not open the Marrocco 2024, CascadeDumpNet 2024, or Rajkumar 2022 full texts; their numbers are second-hand.
- No published YOLOv8/YOLO11 benchmark on TACO with a stated split was found (only a YOLOv5x 88 % mAP@.5 claim at ICCKE 2023 **[unverified]** and unmetriced Roboflow/GitHub demos).

## KQ3. How and on what to train — compute actually used and required (GPU, hours, image sizes, dataset sizes)

### Takeaway
Every published AerialWaste result was obtained with modest hardware: two RTX 2080 Ti (2021 baseline), a single RTX 2080 Ti for inference (2025 pipeline), and a free Google Colab GPU (2508.18315). Recipes converge on ImageNet-pretrained backbones, partial freezing, lr 1e-4–5e-3, early stopping within ~20–100 epochs, inputs of 224–800 px, and a 2:1 negative:positive class ratio; drone-litter detectors are trained at 608–1024 px with COCO initialization for 20–300 epochs.

### Cited Findings
- Torres 2021 (ResNet50+FPN): two RTX 2080 Ti, batch 12, lr 0.005, BCE, early stopping patience 10 / min-delta 0.0005, ImageNet init with first two layers frozen, random flips with p = 0.25, variable 600–1000 px inputs resized to a fixed size. — [Torres 2021 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-04520/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-04520.pdf)
- aerialwaste-model repo: 800 × 800 inputs, lr 0.005, batch 12, patience 10. — [rnt-pmi/aerialwaste-model](https://github.com/rnt-pmi/aerialwaste-model)
- Gibellini 2025: image sizes 200–1048 px depending on GSD × context (e.g., 20 cm/100 m = 500 px; 30 cm/100 m = 332 px; 50 cm/100 m = 200 px; 20 cm/210 m = 1048 px); batch 120; TL lr 1e-3 then FT lr 1e-4 (stage 4 + head); ResNet-50 23 M vs Swin-T 27 M params; Million-AID pretraining (RSP) improves over ImageNet; **inference over 100 km² on one RTX 2080 Ti (12 GB) described as "negligible" relative to human inspection** (no seconds given). — [Gibellini 2025 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)
- 2508.18315: Google Colab GPU; 224 × 224 inputs; batch 16/64; lr 1e-4; ≤100 epochs, patience 20; mixed precision; first 10 layers frozen in the best MobileNetV2 run; train set balanced by augmentation to 4,852 + 4,852. — [arXiv 2508.18315 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)
- Molina 2024: 256 × 256 (512 px "performed similarly"), lr 1e-4 by grid search, first two layers frozen, 17–20 epochs. — [arXiv 2404.01790](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01790v1)
- TACO: Mask R-CNN R50-FPN at 1024 × 1024, 100 epochs, batch 2, SGD lr 0.001, COCO init, augmentation incl. Gaussian blur, noise, exposure/contrast, ±45° rotation, crops, plus 320 "transplanted" instances; "longer training reduces training error but not validation error". GPU not stated. — [TACO PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06975)
- UAVVaste: COCO-pretrained YOLOv3/v4 (and tiny), EfficientDet-d1/d3, MobileNetV2-SSD, inputs 608 × 608, anchors re-clustered; captured by a DJI Mavic Air; training GPU not stated in the sections I read. — [UAVVaste PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-00965/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-00965.pdf); [uavvaste.github.io](https://uavvaste.github.io)
- Majchrowska 2021: EfficientDet-D2 (rwightman PyTorch), lr 1e-3, decay 0.95, 20 epochs, simple resize to default input; DETR ~63 epochs; Mask R-CNN R-50 26 epochs lr 1e-3; classifier EfficientNet-B2 at 224 px, batch 16, lr 1e-4, 20 epochs, pseudo-labelling on OpenLitterMap. — [arXiv 2105.06808 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06808)
- DroneWaste: YOLOv8x / YOLOv12x / Faster R-CNN, COCO-pretrained, 300 epochs with early stopping, rotation/shift/flip/scale augmentation, background-only tiles retained; hardware not stated. — [preprint](https://zenodo.org/records/17288038); [GitHub](https://github.com/lucamora/dronewaste)
- Sun 2023 (BCA-Net): 8 × RTX 3090, training "within a few hours"; annotation took 12 experts six months. — [Sun 2023 full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10015540/fullTextXML)
- Model sizes (torchvision, ImageNet-1k): MobileNet_V2 3.5 M params / 0.3 GFLOPs; GoogLeNet 6.6 M / 1.5; DenseNet121 8.0 M / 2.83; DenseNet201 20.0 M / 4.29; EfficientNet_B0 5.3 M / 0.39; ResNet50 25.6 M / 4.09; ConvNeXt_Tiny 28.6 M / 4.46; Swin_T 28.3 M / 4.49; ViT_B_16 86.6 M / 17.56 (all at 224 px). MobileViT is not in torchvision (it is in `timm`, which 2508.18315's model names `mobilevit_xs`, `vit_tiny_r_s16_p8_224`, `mobilenetv2_050` indicate they used). — [torchvision models](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/stable/models.html); [arXiv 2508.18315 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)
- Colab free tier: "access to expensive resources like GPUs is heavily restricted"; GPU types "vary over time"; limits are not published and "sometimes fluctuate"; runtimes up to 12 h, idle timeouts apply. Third-party guides describe the typical free GPU as a 16 GB T4 **[unverified]**. — [Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html)
- Dataset sizes to download: AerialWaste v3 18.8 GB; v1 18.1 GB; TACO 2.7 GB; UAVVaste 3.0 GB; DroneWaste 3.9 GB; CWLD 6.2 GB; HF Waste-Dumpsites-DroneImagery 1.56 GB. — [Zenodo 12607190](https://zenodo.org/records/12607190); [Zenodo 7034382](https://zenodo.org/records/7034382); [Zenodo 3587843](https://zenodo.org/records/3587843); [Zenodo 8214061](https://zenodo.org/records/8214061); [Zenodo 17045559](https://zenodo.org/records/17045559); [Zenodo 8333888](https://zenodo.org/record/8333888); [HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/INS-IntelligentNetworkSolutions/Waste-Dumpsites-DroneImagery)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** For a single student on free Colab: AerialWaste's 18.8 GB must be staged on Google Drive or down-sampled once (e.g., pre-resize to 256–512 px, which cuts storage ~10×); 2508.18315 is proof that the full v3 set trains on Colab at 224 px. A MobileNetV2/EfficientNet-B0 fine-tune on ~9,000 images at 224 px is minutes per epoch on a T4; ResNet-50/Swin-T at 500 px is roughly 10–20× the FLOPs per image and will need batch ≤ 32 and several hours for 20–30 epochs.
- **[inference]** Choosing 100 m context crops (Gibellini's best) is not possible from the released tiles unless one crops the central 100 × 100 m of each 210 m tile — which the v3 annotation protocol guarantees is label-preserving for positives.
- **[inference]** TACO's 1024-px Mask R-CNN with batch 2 is slow on Colab; Ultralytics YOLOv8n/s at 640 px on TACO or UAVVaste is the practical student path, with the caveat that no peer-reviewed YOLOv8 TACO number exists to compare against.

### Gaps
- No paper reports wall-clock training time for any AerialWaste model.
- UAVVaste and DroneWaste do not state the training GPU, epochs (UAVVaste) or wall-clock time.
- Colab's exact free-tier GPU and quota are unpublished by Google.

## KQ4. Inference speed per image/tile and compute required

### Takeaway
Reported numbers are sparse but consistent: scene classifiers cover 100 km² "negligibly" fast on one RTX 2080 Ti, a Faster R-CNN variant scanned 162 km² of 0.3–1 m imagery in < 30 s on a laptop, and on an NVIDIA Jetson Xavier NX a YOLOv4 at 608 px runs 5.4 FPS (FP32) / 19.3 FPS (FP16) while YOLOv4-tiny reaches 132 FPS (FP16); no inference timing exists for the AerialWaste lightweight classifiers in 2508.18315.

### Cited Findings
- Jetson Xavier NX GPU, 608 × 608, average time per inference (s) / FPS: YOLOv4 FP32 0.1868 / 5.35; FP16 0.0519 / 19.3; INT8 0.0288 / 34.7; YOLOv3 FP16 0.0475 / 21.1; YOLOv4-CSP FP16 0.0429 / 23.3; YOLOv4-tiny-3l FP16 0.0086 / 116.6; YOLOv4-tiny FP16 0.0076 / 132.2, INT8 0.0057 / 176; YOLOv3-tiny INT8 0.0045 / 222.6; EfficientDet-d3 FP16 0.1805 / 5.54 (FP32 "a bit less than three FPS"); EfficientDet-d1 ≈ 9 FPS FP32. Xavier NX CPU / Raspberry Pi 4 CPU: YOLOv4 0.97 / 0.84 FPS, YOLOv4-tiny 11.2 / 8.1 FPS. Coral USB (SSD-MobileNetV2): 23.9–31.7 FPS on USB 3.0. — [UAVVaste PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-00965/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-00965.pdf)
- Gibellini 2025: inference over a 100 km² area on one consumer RTX 2080 Ti (12 GB) "demonstrated to be negligible with respect to the time needed to visually inspect images"; the human study used 125 km² and 1,486 min of manual inspection vs 49.84 km² and 2,133 min AI-assisted (more sites found). — [Gibellini 2025 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)
- Sun 2023: 162 km² test region "in under 30 seconds on a personal laptop"; ~30 cities' central areas (~5,000 km²) in under an hour. — [Sun 2023 full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10015540/fullTextXML)
- SWDet README lists a "Time" column (5.0 and 9.4) next to AP50 with no unit. — [shenhaibb/SWDet](https://github.com/shenhaibb/SWDet)
- 2508.18315 contains no inference timing, FLOPs or parameter table despite listing "evaluating computational efficiency" as a contribution. — [arXiv 2508.18315 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)
- Model FLOPs at 224 px (torchvision): MobileNet_V2 0.3 GFLOPs, EfficientNet_B0 0.39, GoogLeNet 1.5, DenseNet121 2.83, ResNet50 4.09, Swin_T 4.49, ViT_B_16 17.56. — [torchvision models](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/stable/models.html)
- Inspection Tool (rnt-pmi/demo-inspection-tool): a Jupyter/ipyleaflet GUI that displays precomputed tile predictions (score ≥ 0.20) and CAMs over orthophoto/Google Maps layers and exports CSV/KML; it does not itself run the CNN. License CC BY 4.0. — [demo-inspection-tool](https://github.com/rnt-pmi/demo-inspection-tool)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** Tile arithmetic: 100 km² at 210 m tiles ≈ 2,270 tiles; at 100 m tiles = 10,000 tiles. A 0.3-GFLOP MobileNetV2 at 224 px is of the order of 1,000 images/s on a T4-class GPU, so even the 10,000-tile case is seconds; a 4.5-GFLOP Swin-T at 500 px (≈5× the pixels, ≈22 GFLOPs/tile) is on the order of 100 tiles/s, i.e., a couple of minutes per 100 km². The bottleneck is imagery download and tiling, not the network. Sun et al.'s 162 km²/30 s on a laptop (≈430 tiles of 1024 px) is consistent with this.
- **[inference]** For litter-scale detection, throughput is governed by the number of 608–1024 px drone frames, not area; the UAVVaste table shows that FP16 is "free" accuracy-wise while INT8 is not.

### Gaps
- No per-image latency for any AerialWaste classifier, and no FLOPs for MobileViT-XS/ViT-Tiny as used in 2508.18315 (timm models; not in torchvision's table).
- No throughput numbers for DroneWaste, SWDet (unit unknown) or the Polimi pipeline beyond "negligible".

## KQ6. What imagery can inference run on for free, the resolution each target needs, and pretrained aerial weights / foundation models

### Takeaway
For landfill-scale targets, 20–50 cm imagery is sufficient (performance is flat across that range) and is freely available as NAIP (30–100 cm, public domain, US), Spain PNOA (10–25 cm, free with attribution), Netherlands (25 cm, CC BY 4.0), Estonia, Latvia, Switzerland, Luxembourg and some German states; Sentinel-2 (10 m, free Copernicus licence) only supports coarse site-level cues (54 % AP in the one 2026 study) and classifiers trained at 20–50 cm collapse below ~6 m effective GSD. Litter-scale targets need ~2–4 cm/px drone imagery (DroneWaste, UAVVaste) or phone photos. Google Maps Platform content may not be used to train/test/validate/fine-tune ML models or bulk-downloaded; the AerialWaste authors nonetheless shipped Google Earth tiles "subject to Google's terms". RGB-compatible aerial pretrained weights exist in TorchGeo (SatLAS NAIP Swin-V2, SSL4EO/SeCo Sentinel-2 ResNets, Scale-MAE fMoW ViT-L) and as Clay v1.5 (Apache-2.0); Prithvi-EO-2.0 is 30 m HLS and not suited to VHR tiles.

### Cited Findings
**Resolution needed**

### Inferences
- **[inference]** The student's caveat ("non-targeted locations may need commercial high-resolution imagery") is half right: the trained models need ≤ ~50 cm imagery, but that need not be commercial — NAIP (US), PNOA (Spain), Dutch/Estonian/Latvian/Swiss/Luxembourg orthophotos and OpenAerialMap drone mosaics are free; the gap is countries without open orthophoto programmes (where Google Earth is the de-facto source but its ToS forbids ML use and bulk download) and the temporal mismatch (orthophotos refreshed every 2–3 years).
- **[inference]** Running the AerialWaste classifier on Sentinel-2 is a domain shift of ~20–50× in GSD; Molina's results imply it would fail without retraining at that scale, and even retrained models hit ~F1 0.4–0.8 depending on effective pixels per site. For Sentinel-2, a student should reframe to large (> 1 ha) sites and use temporal/NDVI context, as the ISPRS 2026 and Kruse pipelines do.
- **[inference]** SatLAS NAIP Swin-V2-B weights (ODC-BY, aerial RGB at ~1 m) are the closest free "foundation" backbone to AerialWaste's 20–50 cm tiles; Clay (Apache-2.0) is the most permissive general option; Prithvi is the wrong scale.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the licence text for Dutch, Spanish-CNIG-download, French, Danish, Finnish or German national orthophotos directly (portal pages failed or were JS-only); only PNOA-in-GEE and Estonia were read first-hand.
- No published experiment fine-tunes Clay, DOFA or SatLAS weights on AerialWaste; the only remote-sensing pretraining evidence is Million-AID (Gibellini 2025).
- Google's position on training on tiles obtained via Google Earth Pro (desktop) vs the Maps Platform APIs is not spelled out; only the Maps Platform ToS has the explicit ML clause.

## KQ7. Known failure modes: domain shift between sources, class imbalance, "known hotspot" bias, label noise

### Takeaway
Documented failure modes are (1) cross-region shift of −5.1 F1 on average with recall dropping to 77–81 % in Sweden/Greece; (2) collapse when GSD changes by > ~4× without retraining; (3) a 2:1 negative-heavy design that makes accuracy flattering and calibration imperfect (ECE 7 %); (4) positives sourced only from ARPA Lombardia investigations in Lombardy with undisclosed, non-georeferenced locations, so geographic/hotspot bias cannot be audited; and (5) label noise that at least one follow-up paper "fixed" by hand, breaking comparability.

### Cited Findings
- Cross-territory shift: Swin-T trained on Lombardy loses on average 5.10 F1 / 6.83 accuracy points on Greece (WV3), Sweden and Romania (Google Earth); recall falls to 81.03 % / 77.54 % / 87.03 % while precision stays ≥ 90 %; the authors attribute Romania's easier result to "the prevalence of large landfills in the region". — [Gibellini 2025 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)
- Source variability: 2508.18315 notes the three sources' "variability in resolution and quality… hampers consistent performance" and that negatives far outnumber positives; the 2023 paper only shows per-source F1 graphically. — [arXiv 2508.18315 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315); [Torres & Fraternali 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML)
- Resolution shift: a 256-px AerialWaste model applied to 32-px inputs drops from F1 0.86 to 0.20; super-resolution restores ~0.6 but changes the operating threshold. — [arXiv 2404.01790](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01790v1)
- Sampling design / hotspot bias: positives come from ARPA Lombardia's investigation database (105 municipalities in 2021; 487 municipalities in 2023); negatives are drawn 1–5 km from positives to match land use; locations are confidential and images are not georeferenced. — [Torres 2021 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-04520/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-04520.pdf); [Torres & Fraternali 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML)
- Class imbalance by design: negatives = 2 × positives in both train and test "to account for the real class imbalance"; 2508.18315 balanced training by augmentation but kept the imbalanced test and reports a positive-class F1 (88.99) three points below its headline weighted F1. — [Gibellini 2025 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4); [arXiv 2508.18315 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)
- Calibration and errors in the 2021 model: ECE 7.01 on test; false negatives occur when the waste is small or off-centre (CAM attends elsewhere); false positives arise from visually similar negatives (the negative class "represents all the other configurations of the territory", with high inter-class similarity for e.g. construction sites and open fields). — [Torres 2021 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-04520/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-04520.pdf)
- Label noise: 2508.18315 found training images they judged mislabelled in both directions (Figs. 2–3) and moved them between classes before training. — [arXiv 2508.18315 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18315)
- Field precision: in the 2023 deployment, 65 of 69 analyst-found sites were flagged by the mixed-source model (all 69 by the AGEA-only model), and only ~50 % of analyst-confirmed sites scored > 0.75 (mean 0.66), so operating thresholds of 0.2–0.7 are used in practice, trading inspected area for recall (threshold 0.7 cut area by a further 64 % but detections by 32 %). — [Torres & Fraternali 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9889343/fullTextXML); [Gibellini 2025 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06607v4)
- Global-scale detectors show the same precision problem: BCA-Net sensitivity 0.98 but precision 0.70 (0.56–0.59 for agricultural/construction classes); Marrocco et al. report ~40 % false-discovery rate **[unverified]**. — [Sun 2023 full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10015540/fullTextXML); [Marrocco 2024 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3409393)
- Litter detectors fail on small objects: UAVVaste small-object AP ≤ 0.28 even for the best model, and INT8 quantization can zero out accuracy; EfficientDet-D2 APS 9.3 vs APL 59.8. — [UAVVaste PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-00965/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-00965.pdf); [arXiv 2105.06808 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06808)
- The Polimi survey (Waste Management 2024) and the 2025 Remote Sensing thematic review (as summarized by ISPRS 2026) list domain transfer, resolution constraints, and the < 50-pixel small-object problem as open issues and recommend contextual features (road/industry proximity). — [arXiv 2402.09066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09066); [ISPRS 2026 PDF](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/XI-3-2026/849/2026/isprs-annals-XI-3-2026-849-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** Because AerialWaste tiles are not georeferenced and the location list is secret, a student cannot do a geographic (municipality-level) split; the provided split may place neighbouring tiles of the same site in train and test, so reported test numbers are optimistic relative to a true new-territory deployment (the Gibellini generalization table is the better estimate of real-world performance: F1 ≈ 84–91 %).
- **[inference]** A sensible student evaluation protocol: keep the official split, report positive-class precision/recall/F1 and AP (not accuracy), sweep thresholds, and test on a handful of hand-labelled NAIP/PNOA tiles from a new region to expose the shift.

### Gaps
- No paper quantifies per-source (AGEA vs WV3 vs Google Earth) cross-training in numbers; no study tests AerialWaste models on NAIP or other open orthophotos.
- The Roboflow/HF drone dumpsite sets give no geography, so hotspot bias there cannot be assessed.
