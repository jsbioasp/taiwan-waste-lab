# Combining "chemical makeup identification" and "waste/debris detection from imagery": the bridge question (as of 2026-11-06)

Scope note: Raman-spectrum classification, Sentinel-2 marine debris (MARIDA etc.), aerial land waste, and Taiwan data are covered by other researchers. These notes cover only the connective tissue: whether/how the two tasks can share a model, which open data could support that, and what the STARCOP/MethaneMapper "detect a chemical from imagery" recipe implies for plastics.

## A. Physics / feasibility: Raman vs. reflectance spectroscopy, and joint "shared spectral representation" learning

### Takeaway
A Raman classifier (RRUFF/RamanSPy-style, wavenumber axis 400–4000 cm⁻¹, inelastic scattering, lab/handheld point measurement) and a satellite reflectance classifier (VNIR/SWIR 400–2500 nm absorption) see the *same molecules* through *different physics on different axes*; there is no published model that transfers weights from one to the other, and nothing I found attempts it. The only "shared vibrational representation" precedent is Raman + mid-IR (Vib2Mol, 2025–26), which works because both sit on a common wavenumber axis — NIR/SWIR reflectance does not.

### Cited Findings
- Plastic reflectance features in the VNIR/SWIR are overtone/combination bands: NIR absorption in plastics is attributed to "overtones or combination bands of mainly carbon–hydrogen vibrations," with band assignment "challenging" because "overtone vibrations are highly overlapped"; PET's main band at ~1660 nm is assigned to the first overtone of C–H stretching; PE shows bands near 1210 and 1430 nm, PP near 1190 and 1400 nm, PS near 1675 nm; black plastics were excluded owing to "very low reflectance in the NIR" and low SNR — Rani et al., Materials 2019 (MicroNIR plastic sorting) [fetched via Europe PMC full text] — [Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6747759/fullTextXML) (canonical: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6747759)
- Marine-harvested and virgin plastics measured 350–2500 nm (ASD FieldSpec 4) show eight absorption features at ~931, 1045, 1215, 1417, 1537, 1732, 2046, 2313 nm, of which ~931, 1215, 1417, 1732 nm are called diagnostic; the 931 and 1417 nm features "fall outside the atmospheric window" (water-vapour), so from orbit only ~1215 and ~1732 nm are practically usable; the authors suggest convolving library spectra with sensor spectral response functions to simulate satellite signatures; gaps at 1350–1410 and 1800–1950 nm — Garaba & Dierssen, ESSD 12:77–86 (2020) [fetched] — [Source](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/12/77/2020/)
- Water kills the SWIR signal: for submerged plastics reflectance drops sharply at 900–1000 nm; for an orange place mat at 8 cm depth in clear water there was "no measurable reflectance beyond 1134 nm"; in turbid water at 32 cm the residual SWIR signal is from suspended sediment, not plastic — Knaeps et al., ESSD 13:713–730 (2021) [fetched] — [Source](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/13/713/2021/)
- Sentinel-2 pixels are mixed and sub-pixel: "because of their mixed band resolutions and small sub-pixel coverage of debris within a pixel (e.g., <10 %), caution is required when interpreting the spectral shapes of MSI pixels," since distorted spectra could be wrongly treated as endmembers or linked to specific materials — Hu, Marine Pollution Bulletin 183:114082 (2022) [snippet] — [Source](https://ioccg.org/bibliography/hu-c-2022-rsmld-3/)
- Raman and IR share a wavenumber axis and can be fused as channels: Vib2Mol treats Raman and IR spectra as "complementary feature channels" (C=1 unimodal, C=2 joint, concatenated along channel dim), tokenises a 1024-point spectrum with a 1D conv, and uses "masked modality modeling" (joint input 50% of steps, otherwise one modality at random; masked modality replaced by a learnable embedding) so one model serves Raman-only, IR-only, or both; 662,668 theoretical (DFT) spectra + 17,774 experimental (SDBS 2,815 molecules with IR+Raman; NIST-IR 12,144); spectral range 400–4000 cm⁻¹; **NIR/reflectance is not included**; code https://github.com/X1nyuLu/vib2mol, data https://huggingface.co/datasets/xinyulu/vibench; arXiv v1 2025-03-10, v4 2026-01-20 — [fetched] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07014v4)
- Raman-to-Raman transfer learning exists (pretraining on a standard Raman database improved organic-compound classification by ~4–5 points; a 2026 IEEE Access "RSPTE" Raman pre-trained encoder is fine-tuned across tasks) but all stay inside the Raman modality — [snippet] — [Source](https://repositorio.comillas.edu/jspui/retrieve/823814/IIT-26-066R_preview.pdf); [Source](https://remotesensing.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/0/PC123921/Transfer-learning-for-Raman-spectroscopy-in-biological-applications--A/10.1117/12.2651063.full)
- Industry framing of the two modalities: NIR and Raman "provide useful information about chemical composition" for plastics recycling, Raman is highly specific when signal is strong and fluorescence is handled, and "neither method is well suited for identification of black polymers" — Ocean Optics [snippet] — [Source](https://www.oceanoptics.com/?p=4596)
- Complementarity statement: Raman captures the molecular backbone while IR is dominated by functional groups; "weak IR absorbers [are] often strong Raman emitters and vice versa" — Thermo Fisher polymer-analysis deck [snippet] — [Source](https://qa1-assets.thermofisher.cn/TFS-Assets/CAD/Reference-Materials/Polymer-Analysis-Raw-Material-Formulation.pdf)
- A patent asserts Raman spectral features are "significantly sharper and better separated than NIR spectral features" (patent claim, not an independent measurement) — [snippet] — [Source](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6483581)
- Microplastic-ID reviews treat FTIR and Raman as "the primary tools for polymer identification through molecular fingerprinting," with NIR covered only as a secondary technique — [snippet] — [Source](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2023/ra/d3ra05420a); [Source](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s43591-021-00014-y)

### Inferences
- Why a Raman model cannot be reused on reflectance: (1) different physical observable — Raman is a scattered-light *shift* in cm⁻¹ from a laser line, reflectance is *absorption depth vs. wavelength* in nm; (2) different features — reflectance bands are broad, overlapping C–H overtone/combination bands (MicroNIR paper), Raman peaks are fundamentals; (3) different axis — there is no shared coordinate like the wavenumber axis that makes Vib2Mol's Raman+IR fusion possible; (4) different sampling — point measurement of a known object vs. 10–60 m mixed pixels where plastic is <10% of the footprint (Hu 2022), often wet or submerged, which removes the SWIR features entirely (Knaeps 2021). Weight transfer between the two is therefore a category error; what *can* transfer is the *chemical label space* (polymer type) and the *design pattern* (1D spectral encoder + classifier head).
- The physically honest "shared representation" is at the level of *materials*, not spectra: a polymer class predicted by a Raman model and a polymer class predicted from a SWIR reflectance model are the same target variable, so the two tasks can share a label vocabulary and a two-stage pipeline, not a trunk.
- If the instructor wants a true shared-trunk analogue of the tabular project, the honest version is "two reflectance datasets" (lab/field spectral library + satellite pixels), not "Raman + satellite."

### Gaps
- I found no paper that attempts Raman → NIR/SWIR reflectance transfer, nor a "universal vibrational spectrum" model that includes NIR reflectance; Vib2Mol explicitly excludes NIR. State as "none found."
- Moshtaghi et al. 2021 (Sci Rep 11:5436, DOI 10.1038/s41598-021-84867-6; 350–2500 nm, submerged virgin + weathered plastics, open dataset) could not be opened (PMC bot-check); details are from the DOAJ record only — [Source](https://doaj.org/article/e27e0ccfc26e4281bf49a54e68dca650)
- Garaba et al. 2018 airborne-SWIR ES&T paper (NSF PAR purl/10170573) downloaded as binary PDF but could not be text-extracted locally (no pdftotext/pypdf); the "~1215 and ~1732 nm features have potential for detecting ocean plastics" claim is [snippet] — [Source](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10170573)

## B. The real bridge: polymer/material identification from reflectance (open spectral libraries; hyperspectral satellites; Sentinel-2 SWIR) — answers Key Questions 1 and 2

### Takeaway
There *are* open, downloadable VNIR/SWIR reflectance libraries with polymer labels (MADLib 2025 is the largest: 24,889 spectra, CC BY 4.0, ~210 MB CSV; plus Garaba & Dierssen EcoSIS sets, Knaeps 4TU, RODARE polymer HSI cubes, and PLP2021 Sentinel-2 + UAS hyperspectral imagery over known HDPE targets). Polymer-type identification from orbit has been demonstrated once at scale, with EMIT (HDPE vs PVC, matched filter + library, 2025); PRISMA work detects floating plastic but does not resolve polymer type; Sentinel-2 has no band at 1215 or 1732 nm, so its indices (FDI, PI) detect "floating anomaly," not chemistry. EnMAP, PRISMA, and EMIT are free with registration; DESIS commercial services stopped in 2024.

### Inferences
- The spectral-library datasets are small (MADLib 210 MB; Knaeps 5 MB; RODARE 542 MB) and have polymer labels, so they are the practical substrate for a "material class" head; PLP2021 is the only open dataset I found that pairs *satellite* pixels with a *known* polymer (HDPE mesh), but it is 160 GB and the polymer label must be taken from the paper, not the record.
- For a satellite "chemical" head, the only orbital sensors with the 1215/1732 nm windows are EnMAP, PRISMA, EMIT (and the airborne AVIRIS-NG). DESIS lacks SWIR entirely and is therefore irrelevant for polymer ID. Sentinel-2 can at best proxy chemistry through FDI/PI-type contrasts, which Hu (2022) and the 2025 review say are not polymer-specific.
- No hyperspectral-satellite scene set over a *landfill* or *coastal debris site* with polymer labels was found; HyPla would have produced one but has published nothing. A student could, however, pull EnMAP/PRISMA/EMIT L2A over Taiwan coastal or landfill AOIs for free.

## C. STARCOP / MethaneMapper / EMIT as the "detect a specific chemical from imagery" template

### Takeaway
STARCOP and MethaneMapper are verified, downloadable, and both work the same way: a physics-derived *matched-filter enhancement product* (mag1c / HITRAN CH₄ signature) is computed per pixel from hundreds of AVIRIS-NG bands and then a small 2D CNN/transformer segments or boxes the spatial plume. The recipe transfers to plastics only in the sense that Estrela et al. 2025 already used a matched filter + plastic library on EMIT; it does **not** transfer the methane weights, because the "chemical" is baked into the filter, not learned.

- **STARCOP code**: GPL-3.0; `model.model_type='unet_semseg'`; pip-installable; Colab notebooks `model_demos_AVIRIS.ipynb` and `inference_on_raw_EMIT_nc_file.ipynb`; mini demo dataset (STARCOP_mini.zip) and all-bands mini on Hugging Face (previtus/starcop_allbands_mini); pretrained weights at HF isp-uv-es/starcop; full-band AVIRIS access "requires contacting the authors" — [fetched] — [Source](https://github.com/spaceml-org/STARCOP). - **STARCOP paper** (Růžička et al., Sci Rep 13:19999, 2023): HyperSTARCOP = U-Net with MobileNetV2 encoder, ~6.6M parameters, trained from scratch; inputs = mag1c product alone or + RGB (640/550/460 nm); methane absorption ~1600–1850 and 2100–2500 nm; MultiSTARCOP uses WorldView-3's eight SWIR bands in band-ratio products with an ImageNet-pretrained 3-channel encoder; source ≈300 AVIRIS-NG flight lines (~6 TB L1B, Permian Basin), 1,878 plume events balanced with no-plume tiles, 128×128 px training tiles with 64 px overlap → 167,825 images, 512×512 test scenes; AVIRIS-NG 3–10 m GSD; Adam lr 0.001, 15 epochs; **one NVIDIA V100 on a Google Cloud n1-highmem-8, 6–8 h per full train+val run**; F1 up 21.5% (strong plumes) / 8.7% (weak) over mag1c baseline, false-positive rate on no-plume tiles down 41.8%; MultiSTARCOP F1 31.89 vs 7.44 baseline; **EMIT zero-shot** (AVIRIS-trained model, no fine-tune, 11 plume + 9 no-plume locations, native 60 m) gave up to +40.3% F1 with high variance; the "sensor agnostic" claim rests on using the matched-filter product rather than sensor-specific bands — [fetched] — [Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44918-6)
- **MethaneMapper** (Kumar et al., CVPR 2023 Highlight; arXiv 2304.02767): MHS dataset ≈1,185 flightlines (2015–2022) and 3,961 plume sites; each AVIRIS-NG image ≈25,000×1,500×432 bands ≈55–60 GB; tiles 256×256×432 with 128 px overlap; 432 channels at ~5 nm over ~400–2500 nm; Spectral Feature Generator = matched filter projecting each pixel onto the HITRAN methane absorption signature with per-land-cover covariance; a 2000–2500 nm band-pass (~100 channels) feeds a CNN backbone; ResNet-50-based model ≈80M params (vs ≈353M for H-mrcnn); **trained on 2 V100 GPUs for 300 epochs**; 0.63 mAP / 0.91 mIoU on JPL-CH4-detection-v1.0, 0.59 mAP / 0.68 mIoU on MHS; no experiments on other gases/materials; white roofs and asphalt act as confusers; future work "extend the model to global monitoring" with multispectral satellites — [fetched] — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.02767); abstract — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02767)
- **MethaneMapper repo**: "released under the UCSB license" (LICENSE file not opened); includes pretrained weights for plume box detector and segmentation mask, dataset download scripts in `mhs_dataset`, BisQue visualiser; requirements Python ≥3.7, PyTorch ≥1.7, CUDA ≥10; no inference instructions on the README — [fetched] — [Source](https://github.com/UCSB-VRL/MethaneMapper-Spectral-Absorption-aware-Hyperspectral-Transformer-for-Methane-Detection)
- **EMIT "chemical makeup from orbit" products**: L2B mineral identification (Tetracorder, 10 core minerals + others "for transparency", mineral IDs 0–294, 60 m, open) — [fetched] — [Source](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-emitl2bmin-001); L2B CH₄ plume complexes (open) — [snippet] — [Source](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NASA_EMIT_L2B_CH4PLM); EMIT plastics (HDPE/PVC via matched filter + library) — see Section B — [Source](https://earth.jpl.nasa.gov/emit/applications/application-areas/plastics/)

### Inferences
- The STARCOP/MethaneMapper "chemical" is not learned by the network: it enters as a known absorption signature (HITRAN / mag1c). Replacing CH₄ with a polymer means swapping in a plastic library spectrum (Garaba & Dierssen / MADLib convolved to the sensor) as the matched-filter target — which is literally what Estrela et al. 2025 did on EMIT. So the STARCOP recipe *does* apply to plastics, but as "new filter + retrain a 6.6M-param U-Net," not as weight transfer from methane.
- Compute: STARCOP (6.6M params, 1×V100, 6–8 h; mini dataset + Colab notebooks provided) is novice-feasible on Colab if the student uses the mini/strong-plume subsets; MethaneMapper (55–60 GB per flightline, 2×V100 × 300 epochs) is not.
- Licence trap: STARCOP data and weights are CC BY-NC; fine for coursework, not for anything commercial.

### Gaps
- MethaneMapper's exact LICENSE text and MHS download size in GB were not stated on the pages opened.
- STARCOP README does not say how many AVIRIS-NG bands are "selected" in the Zenodo release; the paper's training used the mag1c (+RGB) channels, not the full cube.
- No published attempt to run STARCOP/MethaneMapper-style networks on a *plastic* matched-filter product was found (Estrela 2025 uses the filter without a downstream CNN, as far as the abstract-level sources show).

## D. Architectural combination options, precedents, data needs, Colab feasibility — answers Key Questions 3 and 4

### Takeaway
No paper trains one model for both debris detection and polymer classification from satellite data ("none found" after searching arXiv/IEEE/MDPI/ESSD/GitHub); the closest precedents are (a) a frozen self-supervised Sentinel-2 backbone with parallel task heads that includes MARIDA debris classification (Inzerillo et al. 2024/25), (b) drone/lab hyperspectral pipelines that segment then classify polymer (Guelma 2025; El Bergui 2026), and (c) wavelength-conditioned foundation models (DOFA, HyperSIGMA) that can in principle ingest both Sentinel-2 and hyperspectral pixels. Released foundation-model weights cover Sentinel-2/HLS bands well; only HyperSIGMA (EO-1/GF-5) and DOFA (EnMAP in pretraining) touch hyperspectral.

### Cited Findings

**Option 1 — shared per-pixel 1D spectral trunk, heads for "material class" and "debris vs not"**
- Pixel-wise vs spatial on Sentinel-2: Mifdal, Longépé & Rußwurm (ISPRS Annals 2021) found that "harnessing the spatial patterns learned with a CNN is advantageous over pixel-wise classifications" — i.e., 1D per-pixel models underperform on 10 m MSI debris — [snippet] — [Source](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/V-3-2021/285/2021/)
- Per-pixel ML on Sentinel-2 plastics uses RF/SVM/XGBoost on bands + indices (e.g., Mytilene/Limassol, 54 in-situ plastic samples) — [snippet] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03694)
- 1D/spectral CNNs for *hyperspectral* plastic classification exist at drone/lab scale (El Bergui 2026; Amrita 2026) — see Section B.
- Spectral-library-only training data: MADLib/Knaeps/RODARE (Section B); Vib2Mol-style 1D-conv tokenisation of a spectrum (Section A) is the generic 1D-trunk pattern.

**Option 2 — multi-task segmentation on MARIDA-like data with an auxiliary head**
- Inzerillo, Valsesia & Magli, "Efficient onboard multi-task AI architecture based on self-supervised learning" (arXiv 2408.09754 v2, 2025-07-21; IEEE JSTARS 2024): GhostNetV2 (α=1.6) backbone pretrained with BYOL + local contrastive loss on 590,326 BigEarthNet Sentinel-2 images (4×A100, 550 epochs, batch 3400), then **frozen**; parallel heads for cloud segmentation, flood detection, and **MARIDA** whole-image 11-class multi-label classification (1,381 images 256×256; 694/328/359 split; mF1 68.03 FP32 / 63.98 INT8 vs ResNet50 70.75/33.88, ViT-B/16 64.22/63.74); input 4 bands (R,G,B,NIR); 10.69M params total; runs at 4.6–8.9 Mpx/s on a 7 W Jetson Orin Nano; new tasks = add a head on the frozen features — [fetched] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09754v2)
- MARIDA baselines already use spectral indices as *inputs*; an ISPRS 2022 follow-up extended the index set — [snippet] — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8740969); [Source](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLIII-B3-2022/151/2022/)
- OceanMAE (2026) adds auxiliary ocean descriptors during *pretraining* and reports better MARIDA segmentation — [snippet] — [Source](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.08171)
- Multi-*dataset* (not multi-task) precedent: Rußwurm et al. 2023 (iScience; arXiv 2307.02465) trained a U-Net++ on an aggregation of FloatingObjects + MARIDA + S2Ships + new annotations; gains attributed "mainly to extensive sampling of negative examples and label refinements rather than the specific model architecture" — [snippet] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02465v1); [Source](https://www.github.com/MarcCoru/marinedebrisdetector)
- No paper found that adds a *material/polymer* or *spectral-index regression* head to a MARIDA segmentation network — "none found."

**Option 3 — spectral-spatial models (3D CNN, hyperspectral transformers)**
- HybridSN (Roy et al., IEEE GRSL 2020; arXiv 1902.06701): 3D-CNN spectral-spatial followed by 2D-CNN spatial; Indian Pines / Pavia U / Salinas; official code — [snippet] — [Source](https://github.com/gokriznastic/HybridSN); [Source](https://deepai.org/publication/hybridsn-exploring-3d-2d-cnn-feature-hierarchy-for-hyperspectral-image-classification)
- SpectralFormer (Hong et al., IEEE TGRS 60, 2022; arXiv 2107.02988): transformer with pixel-wise and patch-wise modes; GPL-3.0 — [snippet] — [Source](https://github.com/danfenghong/IEEE_TGRS_SpectralFormer)
- Applied to plastics: El Bergui 2026 patch-CNN + pixel-RF fusion; Guelma 2025 U-Net + SVM (Section B) — both on drone/lab hyperspectral, not satellite.

**Option 4 — remote-sensing foundation models as a shared trunk (Key Question 4: what the weights cover)**
- **DOFA** (Xiong et al., arXiv 2403.15356, 2024): hypernetwork generates patch-embedding weights from each band's central wavelength so one ViT handles optical/SAR/multispectral/hyperspectral; pretrained on >8M images from Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, NAIP, Gaofen, **EnMAP** [snippet from paper/survey] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15356v1); [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20174). Repo: **MIT**; documented checkpoint ViT-Base (`DOFA_ViT_base_e100.pth`, HF XShadow/DOFA; TorchGeo `dofa_base_patch16_224`); wavelengths passed per channel in µm via `wave_list` (e.g., S2 `[0.665, 0.56, 0.49, 0.705, …]`), channel subsets allowed; demos for S1 (2 ch), S2 (9 ch), NAIP RGB; hyperspectral "similar to other images" but no example; GeoBench linear-probe and UPerNet segmentation examples; no compute guidance — [fetched] — [Source](https://github.com/zhu-xlab/DOFA). A TerraTorch zoo cheat-sheet lists DOFA as 300M ViT-L, "dynamic, any band combination," 8M samples — conflicts with the README's ViT-B-only listing — [fetched] — [Source](https://gfms-from-scratch.github.io/extras/cheatsheets/terratorch_model_zoo.html)
- **HyperSIGMA** (Wuhan Univ.; arXiv 2406.11519; IEEE TPAMI Aug 2025 [snippet]): **Apache-2.0**; six checkpoints on HF WHU-Sigma/HyperSIGMA — Spatial_MAE and Spectral_MAE at ViT-B/L/H (`spat-/spec-vit-{base,large,huge}-ultra-checkpoint-1599.pth`); pretraining set HyperGlobal-450K from **EO-1 (1,486 images) and GF-5B (215 images)** with hundreds of bands (README also says "over 20 million three-band images" — unreconciled); pretraining on SLURM with 64–128 DCU accelerators; downstream: classification, target & anomaly detection, change detection, **spectral unmixing**, denoising, super-resolution — [fetched] — [Source](https://github.com/WHU-Sigma/HyperSIGMA); [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11519v2)
- **Prithvi-EO-2.0** (IBM/NASA): **Apache-2.0**; input 6 HLS bands (Blue, Green, Red, Narrow NIR, SWIR1, SWIR2) at 30 m; 4.2M samples; variants 300M, 300M-TL, 600M, 600M-TL (+ tiny 5M, 100M-TL); temporal 3D patch embedding; fine-tune via TerraTorch; example notebooks run on **Colab T4** — [fetched] — [Source](https://huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial/Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M)
- **Clay v1.5**: **Apache-2.0** weights/code; MAE ViT; TerraTorch zoo lists Clay v1 as 100M ViT-B, "dynamic: Sentinel-2, Landsat, NAIP; 1–500 m," 70M samples — [fetched] — [Source](https://huggingface.co/made-with-clay/Clay); [Source](https://clay-foundation.github.io/model/); [Source](https://gfms-from-scratch.github.io/extras/cheatsheets/terratorch_model_zoo.html)
- **SpectralGPT** (Hong et al., IEEE TPAMI 2024; arXiv 2311.07113): 3D-MAE on >1M Sentinel-2 images; weights on Zenodo 10533809 — **CC BY 4.0**, SpectralGPT.pth 1.2 GB and SpectralGPT+.pth 1.2 GB (+ SegMunich 1.4 GB) — [fetched] — [Source](https://zenodo.org/records/10533809); uses 12 S2 bands (B10 excluded) per a third-party HF re-implementation that ships **no original weights** — [snippet] — [Source](https://huggingface.co/OneScience-Group/SpectralGPT); paper — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.07113)
- **SatMAE** (Stanford): fMoW-Sentinel multispectral ViT-B/ViT-L with grouped band patch-embeddings, checkpoints on Zenodo 7338613 (multispectral), 7369796 (temporal), 7369797 (fMoW); licence file present but type not shown; no hyperspectral support — [fetched] — [Source](https://github.com/sustainlab-group/SatMAE)
- Parameter-efficient fine-tuning of Prithvi-2.0-300M and Clay v1 with linear decoders is compared in an IBM/ETH 2025 study — [snippet] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17397v1)

**Option 5 — two-stage pipeline (detect debris in imagery → classify material from spectrum/ground sample)**
- HyPla (GFZ/DLR, 2023–2025) is this exact design at satellite scale (Sentinel-2 CNN → PRISMA/EnMAP material characterisation), unpublished — [fetched] — [Source](https://www.gfz.de/en/section/remote-sensing-and-geoinformatics/projects/hypla)
- Drone/lab scale: Guelma 2025 (U-Net → SVM polymer) and El Bergui 2026 (patch CNN + pixel RF) — [snippet] — [Source](https://dspace.univ-guelma.dz/jspui/handle/123456789/18292); [Source](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/handle/10553/167118)
- Land dumps from Sentinel-2 with a neural network (81 annotated dumps, Buenos Aires) as the "stage 1" baseline — [snippet] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08599)
- Stage-2 orbital chemistry exists only via matched filter + library (EMIT, Estrela 2025) — Section B.

### Inferences
- The instructor's tabular pattern (shared trunk + per-dataset heads + auxiliary targets, deploy to a third country) maps most cleanly onto **Option 2/4**: a Sentinel-2 trunk (Inzerillo's frozen SSL backbone, SpectralGPT, Prithvi, DOFA, Clay) with a MARIDA debris-segmentation head *and* an auxiliary head predicting physically meaningful spectral quantities (FDI, SWIR band depths, or a coarse "plastic-like / vegetation-like / water" material class). The auxiliary target must come from the *same imagery* (indices or PLP2021 HDPE-target pixels), not from Raman.
- The only trunk that could legitimately accept both Sentinel-2 debris tiles and EnMAP/PRISMA/EMIT polymer pixels is a wavelength-conditioned one (**DOFA**, trained with EnMAP among its modalities; or HyperSIGMA for hyperspectral-only). This is research-grade: DOFA gives no hyperspectral example, and HyperSIGMA was pretrained on 64–128 accelerators.
- Novice-on-Colab feasibility ranking: (i) Option 5 with Sentinel-2 detection + a *separate* 1D classifier on MADLib/Knaeps spectra — cheap, all data <1 GB, clearly honest; (ii) Option 2 using a frozen Prithvi/Clay/Inzerillo-style backbone + two heads on MARIDA — Colab T4 notebooks exist for Prithvi; (iii) STARCOP-style small U-Net retrained on a plastic matched-filter product — feasible with the mini set but requires building the filter; (iv) Options 3/4 on hyperspectral satellite data — needs EnMAP/PRISMA/EMIT download, 30–60 m pixels, no polymer labels at scene scale, so a research project, not a course project.
- Key Question 3 answer: **no**, no single model doing both satellite debris detection and polymer classification was found; the nearest are multi-task S2 backbones (debris as one head, no chemistry) and hyperspectral drone pipelines (chemistry, no satellite).

### Gaps
- Inzerillo et al. use MARIDA as *multi-label classification*, not segmentation; no multi-task *segmentation* + material head on MARIDA was found.
- DOFA parameter counts, the full list of five pretraining modalities, and any hyperspectral downstream result were not on the README; the EnMAP-in-pretraining claim comes from the paper/survey snippets.
- HyperSIGMA's handling of arbitrary band counts at fine-tune time and its fine-tune compute were not stated.
- Clay's exact supported sensors/band metadata interface and parameter count were not on the HF card or docs landing page.
- SatMAE and MethaneMapper licence types not confirmed.

## E. Honest verdict framing: real-and-cheap vs research-grade vs category error

### Takeaway
"Combine the two tasks into one" is answerable in three tiers: (1) **real and cheap** — share the *label space* and the *1D-spectral-encoder pattern*, run a two-stage pipeline (S2/aerial detection → separate reflectance-library polymer classifier), or a multi-task Sentinel-2 trunk with a debris head and a physically-grounded auxiliary head; (2) **research-grade** — one wavelength-conditioned trunk (DOFA/HyperSIGMA) fed both Sentinel-2 debris tiles and EnMAP/PRISMA/EMIT pixels, or a STARCOP-style U-Net on a plastic matched-filter product; (3) **category error** — any end-to-end model that ingests Raman spectra and Sentinel-2 imagery into one trunk, or that transfers Raman weights to reflectance.

### Cited Findings
- Physics that makes tier 3 a category error: Raman and IR fuse only because they "share a common x-axis (wavenumber)" — Vib2Mol [fetched] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07014v4); reflectance features are broad, overlapping C–H overtone/combination bands — [fetched] — [Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6747759/fullTextXML); the only orbit-usable plastic features are ~1215 and ~1732 nm — [fetched] — [Source](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/12/77/2020/); Sentinel-2 has no band there (B11 1610, B12 2190 nm) — [Source](https://s2.pages.eopf.copernicus.eu/pdfs-adfs/MSI/L0/PDFS_S2_MSI_L0plus.myst.html); sub-pixel debris (<10%) distorts MSI spectra — [Source](https://ioccg.org/bibliography/hu-c-2022-rsmld-3/); submerged plastic loses all SWIR signal beyond ~1134 nm at 8 cm — [Source](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/13/713/2021/)
- Evidence that tier 1 is real: open polymer-labelled reflectance libraries (MADLib CC BY 4.0, 210 MB; Knaeps CC0, 5 MB; RODARE CC BY 4.0, 542 MB) — [Source](https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/059551d3-2383-4e20-af2d-011c9a59d3ac); [Source](https://data.4tu.nl/articles/_/12896312/2); [Source](https://rodare.hzdr.de/record/3390); frozen-backbone multi-head S2 model with a MARIDA head — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09754v2); Prithvi-EO-2.0 Colab-T4 fine-tune notebooks — [Source](https://huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial/Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M)
- Evidence that tier 2 exists but is heavy: EMIT HDPE/PVC via matched filter + library (orbital polymer ID is possible) — [Source](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/global-scale-detection-of-plastic-from-space-with-the-emit-imaging-spectrometer/1173630948437131270-42); STARCOP 6.6M-param U-Net, 1×V100 6–8 h, zero-shot to EMIT — [Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44918-6); MethaneMapper 2×V100 × 300 epochs on 55–60 GB flightlines — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.02767); HyperSIGMA pretraining on 64–128 accelerators — [Source](https://github.com/WHU-Sigma/HyperSIGMA); DOFA accepts arbitrary wavelengths but documents no hyperspectral example — [Source](https://github.com/zhu-xlab/DOFA); HyPla attempted the two-stage S2→hyperspectral design and published nothing — [Source](https://www.gfz.de/en/section/remote-sensing-and-geoinformatics/projects/hypla)
- Licence constraints to flag to the student: STARCOP data/weights CC BY-NC 4.0 — [Source](https://zenodo.org/records/7863343); [Source](https://huggingface.co/isp-uv-es/starcop); PRISMA no redistribution — [Source](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2021/tech-37E.pdf); EnMAP free incl. commercial — [Source](https://geoservice.dlr.de/resources/licenses/enmap/EnMAP-Data_License.pdf); EMIT unrestricted — [Source](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-emitl2bmin-001)

### Inferences
- "Within the student's selected resources": STARCOP/MethaneMapper are the right *template* (chemical-from-imagery) but the wrong *chemical*; RRUFF/RamanSPy/Liu-2017/DRCNN give the right *architecture pattern* (1D CNN over a spectrum) but the wrong *axis*; MARIDA/FloatingObjects/AerialWaste give the *detection* half. None of the student's resources contain a polymer-labelled reflectance dataset — that is the missing piece, and it is MADLib/Knaeps/Garaba (and PLP2021 for imagery).
- "Without": the closest analogues to the instructor's prior project are Rußwurm 2023 (multi-dataset S2 training with negatives) and Inzerillo 2025 (shared frozen trunk + heads incl. MARIDA); adding a spectral/material auxiliary head to either is a defensible, novel-but-small student project. The "deploy to a third country" step maps to pulling free EnMAP/PRISMA/EMIT or Sentinel-2 over Taiwan AOIs.
- Recommended framing for feedback: the student should replace "combine Raman + satellite" with "share a material vocabulary across a reflectance-library classifier and an imagery debris detector," and should explicitly state that Raman stays a ground-truthing instrument (two-stage), not an input modality.

### Gaps
- No quantitative report exists (that I found) of how much a material/spectral auxiliary head helps MARIDA segmentation — the proposal is untested in the literature.
- No open EnMAP/PRISMA/EMIT scene collection with debris/landfill polymer labels exists; a student would have to self-label using MADLib-convolved signatures or PLP2021 HDPE targets.
