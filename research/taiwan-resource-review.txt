# Taiwan teams must build their labels

Most of the benchmark datasets behind today's chemical and waste detection models are free to download, but only a few can legally train a commercial product. Among the remote-sensing sets, **MARIDA and MADOS (marine debris, CC BY 4.0) are the only reviewed waste datasets cleared for commercial use**. STARCOP (CC BY-NC), MARS-S2L (CC BY-NC-SA), AerialWaste (CC BY-NC-ND) and the Sun et al. global dumpsite set (academic only) all bar it. On the spectral side the picture is murkier: RRUFF is licensed "for educational purposes", MLROD's code is CC BY-NC, and Bacteria-ID, API and MHS state no license at all, so treat them as permission-required. None of the reviewed datasets contains a confirmed Taiwan sample. Taiwan's own open data is rich in **geocoded label lists** (MOENV's monthly list of dumping sites with coordinates, OCA beach-cleanup tonnage, landfill coordinates, hourly NMHC readings) and nearly empty in **labelled imagery or spectra**: the only public Taiwan aerial litter dataset is NTOU's small HAIDA set, and no open Taiwan Raman, FTIR or hyperspectral library turned up. Methane is a special case. Taiwan has no fossil-fuel extraction, landfill methane has collapsed since the 1990s, and no public satellite plume over Taiwan was found, so a methane module will lean on ground stations and free EMIT/Carbon Mapper products rather than local training data. The practical path is to train on CC BY and public-domain sources, use Taiwan government lists as weak labels over free Sentinel imagery, and collect your own drone imagery and reference spectra for the last mile.

Several Taiwan government portals (data.gov.tw, data.moenv.gov.tw, cca.gov.tw) blocked automated access during research, so some Taiwan items rest on mirrors or search titles. These are flagged in the tables and collected in the gaps section. License readings here are not legal advice.

## Two of eleven reviewed benchmarks allow commercial training

The reviewed models drew on four families of data. The table below gives the commercial-use verdict first, because that is what decides whether a dataset can go into a product or only into a research prototype.

### Chemical spectra (Raman, IR)

| Dataset | What it is | Size and host | License | Commercial use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RRUFF | Raman, IR, XRD and chemistry for minerals, ASCII spectra with rich metadata | Bulk zips on rruff.info, migrating to rruff.net | "For educational purposes", credit required ([rruff.info](https://rruff.info/about/about_download.php)) | Unclear, ask the owners |
| RamanSPy built-in sets | Loaders only: Bacteria, THP-1 cells, MDA-MB-231, COVID-19 serum, adenine SERS, wheat, RRUFF | Dropbox, Zenodo, Google Drive, Kaggle | None stated for any set ([RamanSPy docs](https://ramanspy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/datasets.html)) | Unknown per set |
| Bacteria-ID (Ho et al. 2019) | 80k+ spectra, 30+ isolates, 633 nm, plus clinical splits | Authors' Dropbox, manual download | Not verified | Unknown |
| MLROD (Berlanga et al. 2022) | Mars-analog minerals, 532 nm, 89,121 training and 39,720 test spectra, 15 classes ([arXiv 2601.16107](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16107)) | Possibly NASA AHED (unverified) | Code is CC BY-NC ([Zenodo 7036374](https://zenodo.org/record/7036374)); data license unconfirmed | No (code); data unknown |
| API Raman (Flanagan & Glavin 2025) | 3,510 spectra of 31 pharmaceutical compounds at 785 nm ([arXiv 2601.16107](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16107)) | Repository not verified | Not verified (Scientific Data usually requires open terms) | Probably, unconfirmed |
| KnowItAll (Wiley) | 28,000+ Raman spectra in 33 databases ([Wiley](https://sciencesolutions.wiley.com/?p=790)) | Subscription by quote | Commercial EULA, ML terms not public | Paid, check EULA |

The 2026 benchmark by Sineesh and Kamsali trained Deep CNN, SANet, RamanNet, Transformer, RamanFormer, Random Forest and SVC models on MLROD, Bacteria-ID and API, and it **states no license for any of the three** ([arXiv 2601.16107](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16107)). That is typical of the field. One time-sensitive warning: rruff.info now says its data is about three years out of date and the site will go offline, with new data going only to the RRUFF.net beta ([rruff.info](https://rruff.info/index.php)). Any pipeline that relies on RamanSPy's `rruff()` loader, which points at rruff.info, should expect it to break.

### Methane plumes

| Dataset | What it is | Size and host | License | Commercial use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STARCOP | AVIRIS-NG hyperspectral tiles with refined plume masks, plus simulated WorldView-3 views | About 61 GB, Zenodo, open ([Zenodo 7863343](https://zenodo.org/records/7863343)) | Data CC BY-NC 4.0, code GPL-3.0 ([GitHub](https://github.com/spaceml-org/STARCOP)) | No |
| Methane Hot Spots (MHS) | About 1,185 AVIRIS-NG flight lines, 3,961 plume sites in six US states, masks, boxes and ppm maps ([arXiv 2304.02767](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02767)) | Likely several TB; download path not verified | Code under a "UCSB license"; data license unstated ([GitHub](https://github.com/UCSB-VRL/MethaneMapper-Spectral-Absorption-aware-Hyperspectral-Transformer-for-Methane-Detection)) | Unknown |
| MARS-S2L (UNEP-IMEO) | About 87,000 Sentinel-2/Landsat pairs, 5,600+ verified plumes, CH4Net checkpoint | About 100 GB, Hugging Face, ungated | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, explicitly covering models too ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UNEP-IMEO/MARS-S2L/blob/main/README.md)) | No |

STARCOP's HyperSTARCOP model beat a matched-filter baseline by more than 25% F1 ([Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-44918-6)), so it is the strongest reference, but it is non-commercial. MARS-S2L is notable because it **extends the NC-SA terms to its pre-trained weights**, which removes the usual "weights are not derivatives" argument.

### Marine debris

| Dataset | What it is | Size and host | License | Commercial use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MARIDA | 1,381 Sentinel-2 patches, 15 classes, 63 scenes in 11 countries including China, Philippines, Vietnam, South Korea, Indonesia | 1.2 GB zipped, Zenodo ([Zenodo 5151941](https://zenodo.org/record/5151941)) | CC BY 4.0 | Yes, with attribution |
| MADOS (MARIDA successor, 2024) | 2,803 patches from 174 scenes, adds oil spill, oil platform, jellyfish | 4.0 GB, Zenodo ([Zenodo 10664073](https://zenodo.org/records/10664073)) | CC BY 4.0 | Yes, with attribution |
| FloatingObjects (ESA Φ-lab) | 26 Sentinel-2 scenes, 3,297 floating objects as noisy line labels ([arXiv 2307.02465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02465)); includes Manila and three Vietnam scenes | Google Drive | No license file ([GitHub](https://github.com/MarcCoru/floatingobjects)) | Unknown |
| marinedebrisdetector combo | FloatingObjects + MARIDA + S2Ships, refined labels | GitHub | None named | Inherits weakest license |

### Landfills and dumpsites

| Dataset | What it is | Size and host | License | Commercial use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AerialWaste v3 | 11,700+ aerial and satellite images, binary plus multi-label plus masks | 18.8 GB, Zenodo ([Zenodo 12607190](https://zenodo.org/records/12607190)) | CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, plus Google Earth terms | No, and no modified redistribution |
| Sun et al. 2023 global dumpsites | About 2,500 dumpsites in 2,219 tiles at 0.3 to 1 m, including Fujian | Science Data Bank, URL not given | "Academic use only, commercial use prohibited" ([Nature Comms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-37136-1.pdf)) | No |

One structural point matters more than any single license. The underlying Sentinel-2 and Landsat imagery is free and open, so **the NC restrictions attach to the labels and curation, not the pixels**. A team that re-labels raw imagery itself sidesteps the restriction, which is exactly why Taiwan government location lists become valuable later in this report.

Geography is the other gap. MARIDA's Chinese and Philippine scenes and FloatingObjects' Manila and Vietnam scenes are the closest analogues to Taiwan's typhoon-driven river plumes, and the Sun et al. dumpsites include Fujian across the strait ([Nature Comms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-37136-1.pdf)), but none contains Taiwan. MADOS's 47 tiles were not checked for Taiwan Strait coverage.

Taiwan has a regulatory hook but no library. MOENV's standard method **NIEA M907.00B** identifies microbeads in cosmetics with ATR-FTIR (550 to 4000 cm⁻¹) confirmed by Raman, against just six polymers (PE, PP, PET, PMMA, nylon, PLA) at ≥80% match, and it tells labs to build their own reference library rather than publishing one ([Executive Yuan Gazette](https://gazette.nat.gov.tw/EG_FileManager/eguploadpub/eg023216/ch07/type1/gov60/num22/images/Eg01.pdf)). A repeal and replacement were pre-announced, and a water microplastics method (M909.00C) appears to be pyrolysis-based, though neither page could be opened. Taiwan microplastic field studies (northern beaches, seafood) exist, but searches of Zenodo, Figshare and Mendeley found **no Taiwan-affiliated open spectra**. The data lives in papers and theses.

## Taiwan publishes label lists, not labelled imagery

This is the most useful finding for a Taiwan team. Government portals hold dozens of geocoded or county-level records that can serve as weak labels, hotspot priors or validation, all under the **Open Government Data License v1 (政府資料開放授權條款)**, free of charge. Metadata below was confirmed through data.gov.tw's English mirror or NCHC SciDM unless flagged.

| Dataset (ID) | Provider | Key fields | Use for detection | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Listed waste disposal sites 列管廢棄物棄置場址 (145968) | MOENV | Case no., address, cadastre, **Lon/Lat**, waste kind, listing date, cleanup status, monthly updates ([data.gov.tw](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/145968)) | Positive labels for illegal-dump detectors; status allows "cleared vs present" time series | Verified (metadata last stamped 2024-10) |
| Taoyuan sanitary landfills (76762) | Taoyuan | Name, capacity, remaining volume, **Lat/Lon** ([data.gov.tw](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/76762)) | Legal-waste positives or hard negatives | Verified |
| Tainan illegal disposal sites (173490) | Tainan | Site no., district, location name, no coordinates ([data.gov.tw](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/173490)) | Needs geocoding | Verified |
| Soil and groundwater contaminated sites (8817, 6351, 35094) | MOENV | Site map, names and addresses, counts and areas ([SciDM](https://scidm.nchc.org.tw/en/dataset/best_wish8817)) | Industrial-site positives and negatives | Listed, but mirrors show "no data"; live access via data.moenv.gov.tw unverified |

| Floating debris and microplastics monitoring (175841) | OCA | Survey fields and report ([data.gov.tw](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/175841)) | Offshore hotspot validation | Verified, no download link seen |
| Tainan beach cleanup results (137782) | Tainan (from MOENV adoption system) | API with Swagger ([data.gov.tw](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/137782)) | Local validation | Verified, fields empty on page |
| NMHC hourly values (34829) | MOENV | Hourly NMHC, daily update ([SciDM mirror](https://scidm.nchc.org.tw/ja/rating/dataset/best_wish34829/4)) | Ground-truth near industrial sites | Verified listing; CH4 field and station count unconfirmed |
| National GHG Inventory Report (16024) | CCA | Sector emissions ([data.gov.tw](https://data.gov.tw/dataset/16024)) | Context | Unverified (blocked) |

Three caveats shape how these lists can be used. Coordinates in 145968 are likely cadastral centroids, so expect positional error of tens of metres. The list is biased toward large, known industrial dumps, and a 2021 PTS report citing MOENV found 870 historical sites with 323 still supervised, while reporters said listed sites are only a fraction of the real total, especially on farmland and fish ponds ([PTS](https://news.pts.org.tw/article/519007)). And none of the marine data is imagery.

Taiwan's labelled imagery is thin. **HAIDA** from NTOU is the only public Taiwan aerial litter dataset found: **1,319 drone images plus 456 negatives, 6,421 boxes in two classes (2,517 bottle, 3,904 garbage)**, flown at 3 to 30 m along the Keelung and Gongliao coast with about 0.7 cm/px at 10 m ([MDPI Aerospace](https://mdpi.com/2226-4310/10/1/84)). The repo is MIT-licensed, but the LICENSE file is inherited from the keras-yolo3 fork and names that author, and the images sit on personal Google Drive links ([raw LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LiaoSteve/HAIDA-Trash-Dataset-High-Resolution-Aerial-image/master/LICENSE)). Treat it as permissive but ambiguous until the authors confirm. Its centimetre-scale resolution also means it will not transfer to orthophotos or satellites without domain adaptation.

Other Taiwan work does not release data. NCU's Taoyuan drone bottle study ([MDPI Drones](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/6/12/401)), NCKU's 2024 YOLO debris thesis, and Tamkang's CCTV littering detector all report no public dataset. Government illegal-dumping enforcement is moving toward IoT and CCTV, not imagery: MOENV's NT$2.35B 2026 to 2029 plan funds 500 IoT monitoring sites in its first year ([優分析](https://uanalyze.com.tw/articles/4746544570)), and the June 2026 Waste Disposal Act amendments add electronic fences and CCTV ([工商時報](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260617700105-430104)). **No Taiwanese satellite or drone illegal-landfill detection paper was found**, so a product here would be locally novel.

Citizen-science beach data is the best ground truth for class mix. SOW's ICC records span 541 cleanups from 2004 to 2016, 904,302 items and 63.6% plastic ([AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/ar/records/65dfd095b766d82b18033432)), and its 121-station coastal survey (2018) and 14-station re-survey (2022) identified Ruifang, Mailiao, Guanyin, Tongxiao and Manzhou as the dirtiest segments ([SOW PDF](https://www.sow.org.tw/sites/sow/files/xin_wen_gao__huang_ye_qi_dong_hai_fei_fu_cha__jian_shi_tai_wan_hai_fei__fa_xian_bu_fen_re_dian_bu_jian_fan_zeng_.pdf)). That station data is available only on request. The National Ocean Database (NODASS) has an open tier under the OGDL and a controlled tier that **bans commercial use** ([release rules](https://www.klsh.kl.edu.tw/wp-content/uploads/sites/36/2023/05/國家海洋資料庫資料釋出作業要點.pdf)), and no debris layer was found in it.

## Taiwan's methane is diffuse, so detection targets are few

The methane module faces a target problem before it faces a data problem. EPA figures cited in 2022 put **methane at 1.67% of Taiwan's GHG emissions**, with 2019 methane at half of 2005's. Landfills produced at least 85% of methane in the late 1990s but have fallen to a negligible level, rice paddies now produce more than a quarter, pig manure is another major source, and Taiwan has no significant fossil-fuel extraction ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2022/01/26/2003772081)). The 2026 inventory (covering 2024) reports net emissions of 251.404 Mt CO2e ([Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202606030011)) and a **72.47% drop in the waste sector since 2005** ([經濟日報](https://money.udn.com/money/story/7307/9542944)). No opened source gave the 2024 methane or landfill figure.

Searches found **no public satellite methane plume over Taiwan** from Carbon Mapper, IMEO MARS, EMIT, GHGSat or Kayrros, and the bounding-box query could not be run. That is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence. The most plausible point sources are LNG terminals (Yong'an, Taichung), petrochemical complexes (Mailiao, Linyuan) and large legacy landfills, but this is reasoning, not a finding. The one Taiwan-specific methane observation study, a 2023 NCU thesis using GOSAT, TROPOMI and ground stations, found industrial areas show larger day-night swings and mixed local and regional sources, without naming any facility ([NCU IR](https://ir.lib.ncu.edu.tw/bitstream/987654321/92086/1/index.html)).

Ground stations are the realistic Taiwan data source. Selected MOENV air quality stations measure total hydrocarbons by FID, with NMHC computed as THC minus methane ([NCU thesis](https://ir.lib.ncu.edu.tw/ir/bitstream/987654321/89119/1/index.html)), so those stations do produce a methane value. A separate PAMS network sums 56 speciated hydrocarbons and has picked up petrochemical signatures at industrial sites ([NCU Scholars](https://scholars.ncu.edu.tw/zh/publications/network-monitoring-of-speciated-vs-total-non-methane-hydrocarbon-/)). Whether methane appears as its own API field, and at how many stations, is unconfirmed. Civil IoT Taiwan showed no methane sensors.

## Conclusion

The bottleneck for a Taiwan detection product is not model architecture or even data volume. It is **label ownership**. The global benchmarks taught the field how to detect plumes, debris and dumps, but most of them cannot ship, and none of them sees Taiwan. Meanwhile Taiwan's government has already done the expensive part of ground truthing, recording where dumps, landfills, contaminated sites and beach litter actually are, and released it under an open license. Pairing those coordinates with free Sentinel imagery, then adding a modest amount of owned drone imagery and in-house spectra, produces a training set that is both commercially clean and locally accurate, which no public dataset offers.

The modules also deserve different ambitions. Illegal-landfill detection is the strongest opportunity: good labels exist, enforcement policy is moving toward technology, and no local competitor was found. Marine debris is viable on MARIDA and MADOS with Taiwan validation. Spectral material ID needs a self-built Taiwan library and is a lab-workflow product more than a data product. Methane should be framed as monitoring with ground stations and free satellite plume products, because Taiwan's emission profile offers few large plumes to train on or find.
